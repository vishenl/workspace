The Hayden Story

4 min Slides 3-5
Vishen's First Social Media Video

"Ten years ago, I was already running a $40 million company. I was putting authors in front of cameras. I was building Mindvalley into something real. And I was terrified of posting on Facebook."

"So I came up with a hack. Instead of posting for the world - because who the fuck cares about me, right? - I would make videos for my son Hayden. That way, if anyone criticized me, I could say: 'Hey, I wasn't talking to YOU. I was talking to my six-year-old.'"

"There was just one problem..."
"My son was six. He didn't give a shit either."
Timing: Pause after "one problem" - let anticipation build. Deliver punchline with a slight head shake and self-deprecating smile.
SLIDE 3 "This is literally how my videos started: 'Hey Hayden, check it out...'"
"I'd start every video with 'Hey Hayden' - and I'd end every video with 'Miss you, Hayden. See you soon.'"
"That way, nobody could tell me I sucked. Because technically, I wasn't talking to them."
Timing: Lean in like sharing a secret. Make air quotes around "technically."
Show vulnerability here. This isn't bragging about growth - it's admitting how scared you were.
SLIDE 4-5 "I went from 30,000 followers to 1.1 million. And it didn't happen because I got less scared. It happened because I built a system."
"Let me show you what changed everything."

The New Founder Era

5 min Slides 6-13
SLIDE 6 "Part One: The New Founder Era."

The Three Truths:

  1. People buy what you stand for, not what you sell
  2. Your company's brand grows only as far as your own evolution
  3. You are your company's greatest marketing weapon
SLIDE 8 "Here's a stat that blew my mind. My personal Instagram gets three times the engagement of Mindvalley's company account. Three times. And we have a professional social team running that account."
"People don't want to follow companies."
"They want to follow humans. Even weird ones."

(point to self) "Especially weird ones, apparently."
Timing: Pause after "humans." Then add "Even weird ones" with a slight smile. The self-point comes after audience laugh.

Ask: "How many of you have a company social account that gets more engagement than your personal one?"

Wait for hands - usually very few

"Exactly. The world has shifted. We just haven't shifted with it."

Nora Cavani - Female Founder of the Year

"Let me tell you about Nora. She runs Alba Health - it's a gut microbiome testing company for kids. Basically, parents send in a swab of their kid's poop, and she tells them what's actually going on in their child's health."

"So her company analyzes children's poop."
"And she just won Female Founder of the Year in Europe. Goes to show - there's gold in them hills."
Timing: Deadpan the first line. Pause. Then deliver "gold in them hills" with raised eyebrows.
"Nora was about to go on stage at a major conference. She had her pitch deck ready - all about the technology, the science, the testing process. Fifteen minutes before she went on, she threw it all out."
"Instead of pitching technology, she pitched a movement. 'I'm not just selling gut tests. I'm building a movement for children's health. Big pharma keeps our kids sick with prescriptions when the real problem might just be fiber.'"
SLIDE 12 "The results: Sales tripled. Her website got more traffic in one week than the previous three months combined. Her team was packing orders until 11pm."

Don't build a product. Build a movement.

Before you think about tactics, ask: What movement am I building? What do I stand for that's bigger than my product?

"Now let's talk about where you build that movement."

The Founder Media Ecosystem

5 min Slides 14-21
SLIDE 14-15 "I brought in an advisor named Sabrina Stocker. She's 29, was on The Apprentice UK, and she completely rewired how I think about platforms. She taught me that each platform is a different room with different rules."

The Four Rooms:

  • YouTube = The Classroom — Educate and inspire. Coffee conversation energy.
  • LinkedIn = The Boardroom — Lead with vulnerability. Text beats video.
  • Instagram = The Cafe — Entertainment first. Side-angle camera.
  • Newsletter = The Sanctuary — You OWN this list. No algorithm can throttle you.
Walk through each platform with hand gestures - physically point to different parts of the room as if placing each platform there.
SLIDE 16 "On YouTube, you talk straight to camera. Like we're having coffee. You're the teacher, they're the student."
SLIDE 17 "On LinkedIn, something weird happens: text beats video. People are browsing at work. They can't watch a video with their boss walking by. So you write. And the winners? They don't share their wins - they share what went wrong."
SLIDE 18 "On Instagram, it's entertainment. The camera is to the side - like you're being interviewed on a talk show. People are scrolling for dopamine, so you give them emotion."
SLIDE 19 "And newsletter - this is your sanctuary. Unlike every other platform, you OWN this list. Instagram can throttle you. YouTube can demonetize you. But your email list? That's yours forever."
SLIDE 20 "Here's a pro tip. Record with two cameras at once. Front camera for YouTube. Side camera for Instagram."
"One recording. Two pieces of content. That's not lazy - that's leverage."
Timing: Wink on "That's not lazy" - make it clear you're being playful.

Ask: "Which platform should you choose?"

Pause for audience to think

SLIDE 21 "The one you'll actually use. That's it. Most of us know what we should do. The question is whether we'll do it."

"Now let's talk about something that's going to change everything in the next 12 months."

Vector Science & AEO

4 min Slides 22-25
SLIDE 22-23 "There's a new game being played, and most people don't even know it exists. It's called AI Engine Optimization - or AEO. And it's about how AI decides who gets found."

Vector Science: AI doesn't just search for keywords. It creates associations. It builds vectors - invisible lines connecting you to concepts.

When someone asks AI "Who are the best speakers on personal growth?" - your name either appears... or it doesn't. And that depends on the associations you've built.

"I asked ChatGPT who I was."
"It got my birthday wrong, said I founded Mindvalley in the wrong year, and mentioned a book I never wrote. But hey - at least it knew I existed."
Timing: Build frustration with the list of errors. Throw hands up on "at least it knew I existed."
SLIDE 25 "Here's the critical window: 6 to 12 months. Those who understand vector science now will be exponentially ahead. And once that gap opens up... it will be very hard to catch up."
Lean in with urgency here. This should feel like sharing insider information.
"But here's the thing - AI can only help you if it knows who you are."

Personal Brand Operating System

6 min Slides 26-31
SLIDE 27 "Have you ever typed a prompt into ChatGPT asking it to write a LinkedIn post for you?"
"And it comes back sounding like a motivational poster had a baby with a corporate memo? And you close the tab in disgust?"
Timing: Make a disgusted face. Mime closing a laptop aggressively.
"The problem isn't AI. The problem is AI doesn't know you yet. You need to build your Personal Brand Operating System first."

The 5-Step Personal Brand OS:

  1. Your Story — Go for a coffee walk. Record your life story with Otter AI.
  2. Online Identity — Feed AI your existing content so it knows your voice.
  3. Role Models — Who do you want to be like online? Your top 5-10.
  4. Boundaries — What do you NOT want to share?
  5. Vision & Goals — What are you actually trying to achieve?
SLIDE 30 "Step one: Go for a coffee walk. Use Otter AI or just the voice memo on your phone. Tell your life story. Childhood. What made you angry. What excited you. How you ended up here."
The Coffee Walk

"The first time I did this, I walked for 45 minutes around my neighborhood. I talked about growing up in Malaysia. I talked about my dad's philosophy. I talked about the exact moment I decided to leave my job at Microsoft. By the end, I had forgotten I was recording."

"That's when the gold comes out - when you forget you're performing."

SLIDE 31 "Step four is boundaries. What do you NOT want to share?"
"For me, it's pictures of my kids. I don't share them. And I made that very clear to my AI. Now when it suggests content ideas, it never suggests 'Post a cute photo of your kids!' Because it knows I'll throw my laptop across the room."
Timing: Start serious, then exaggerate the "throw my laptop" for comedic effect.
"Now let's get tactical. Platform by platform."

Platform Strategies

10 min Slides 32-43

YouTube Strategy

The Daniel Priestly Model

"Daniel Priestly has built $700 million in companies. His YouTube strategy? He sits in front of a camera and teaches. That's it. No fancy production. No B-roll of him walking on a beach. Just Daniel, talking to camera, like you're across the table."

SLIDE 34 "Result: 84,000 views on videos with only 61,000 subscribers. That ratio is insane."

SLIDE 35 "I made a video about ChatGPT. It got 700,000 views on Instagram. Want to guess how it did on YouTube?"
"4,200 views. Same video. Same content. Different platform."
Timing: Let audience guess. Then deliver "4,200" with deadpan disappointment.
"The problem was the title. I called it 'The Rise of ChatGPT.' That's a documentary title, not a YouTube title."
SLIDE 36 "A better title? 'The #1 Reason You'll Be Left Behind by AI in 2026.' Now THAT makes you click."

The Iman Gadzhi Model: Your educational video naturally leads to your offer. At the end, invite them to watch a "30-minute deep dive" - which is actually your sales presentation.

First video = Content. End CTA = Offer.

LinkedIn Strategy

SLIDE 38 "On LinkedIn, the biggest winners aren't sharing their wins. They're sharing what went wrong."
"I used to share posts like: 'Just hit $100 million in revenue! Here's what I learned!'"
"Those got like 47 likes. From my mom. And employees who were afraid of getting fired."
Timing: Fake enthusiasm on the first line. Then deflate completely on "47 likes."
The Fake Name Story

"When I first started selling meditation CDs online, I was so embarrassed that I used a fake name. My friends from Michigan were becoming lawyers and consultants. I was selling MP3s about breathing. I literally hid behind a pseudonym because I was afraid my friends would find out."

"THAT story? That gets engagement. Because it's vulnerable. It's real. It's messy."

The Likability Principle (Cialdini): People buy from people they like. And people like you because of:

  • Vulnerability
  • Authenticity
  • Humor

Newsletter Strategy

The $2.5 Million Newsletter

SLIDE 40 "In December, I started writing a weekly newsletter. Real stories. A dinner conversation with Paul McKenna. A photo my son took. Super personal stuff."

SLIDE 41 "The year before I started: email revenue down 38%. After I started: email revenue UP 44%."
SLIDE 42 "I did the math. I wrote 45 newsletters. We made an extra $2.5 million. That's $56,000 per newsletter."
"And here's the crazy part: the newsletter doesn't sell anything."
"There's no pitch. No 'click here to buy.' It just builds trust. And when I eventually do offer something... they're ready."
Timing: Let "doesn't sell anything" land with surprise. Then explain the mechanism.
SLIDE 43 "An email subscriber is worth more than a follower. Because no algorithm can throttle your list. You own it."
"Now let me show you how all of this connects."

The Content Flywheel

4 min Slides 44-46
SLIDE 45 "One piece of content. Multiple platforms. Maximum leverage."

The Flywheel:

  1. Write your newsletter (core content)
  2. Extract LinkedIn posts (each lesson = one post)
  3. Turn it into a video script with AI
  4. Record from two angles (front for YouTube, side for Instagram)
  5. Create clips for TikTok/Instagram Reels
  6. Send reflection email to continue the conversation
"Some people call this 'repurposing.' I call it..."
"...working smarter while everyone else is burning out creating 47 pieces of original content a week."
Timing: Pause like you're searching for a profound word. Then deliver the long punchline with mock seriousness.
"Start with email. It's the least sexy. But it's the most valuable. Then let the flywheel do the rest."
"One more thing before we close."
