PART ONE

The Visibility Wound

Why founders hide—and how to heal

ORIGIN STORY

"Hey Hayden, check it out..."

In 2015, I stood outside Steve Jobs' house in Silicon Valley. I'd been running Mindvalley for 10 years—$30-40 million in revenue, no venture capital. But I couldn't bring myself to share on social media.

My hack? I recorded videos for an audience of one: my six-year-old son Hayden. "Hey Hayden, check it out..." Because if I was just talking to my kid, nobody could tell me I sucked.

The funny thing? My son didn't really care. But I ended every video with "Miss you, Hayden. See you soon." That way, nobody could judge me.

The Visibility Block

This is called the visibility wound—a fear of being seen. It's shockingly common among founders. My newsletter article "Do You Have a Visibility Wound?" got more reads than anything I'd written in six months.

The wound shows up as: "Who am I to share this?" "What will people think?" "I'm not an expert." "My friends are lawyers and consultants—I sell meditation CDs."

"I had built a company for 10 years. I was a good founder. But I was afraid to let the world see me."

The Jay Shetty Breakthrough

In 2017, at a Mindvalley event in Sardinia, I told Jay Shetty: "I'm 44 years old. I'm too old. I don't have your hair. I don't know how to dress like you."

Jay said: "V, it's all bullshit excuses. You're fine. Just start."

That week, I posted my first real Instagram post—a joke about a bad haircut. From 30,000 followers, I grew to 1.1 million. And here's the crazy part: my personal Instagram gets 3x more engagement than the Mindvalley company account.

Why This Matters Now

The world has shifted. We don't want to follow companies anymore—we want to follow individuals. As a founder, you are your company's greatest marketing weapon.

Richard Branson became the face of Virgin

Mark Zuckerberg stepped out as the face of Meta

Elon Musk IS Tesla in the public mind

People invest in companies where they can see the founder

People buy from founders they trust

Your personal brand = Your company's value

PART TWO

The New Founder Era

Build movements, not just products

The Shift

Founders no longer just build companies. They build movements.

People buy what you stand for, not just what you sell. Your company's brand can only grow as far as your own evolution.

CASE STUDY: NORA CAVANI

From Product Pitch to Movement

Nora Cavani, founder of Alba Health (a children's gut microbiome testing company), was about to pitch at a London tech event. She was supposed to talk about "technology" and "testing."

Fifteen minutes before going on stage, she remembered a lesson from Mindvalley: "Build bigger than yourself."

Instead of pitching technology, she spoke about a movement: "I'm building a movement around healthy guts for kids. Your child may not be sick—their microbiome is sick. Big pharma keeps children on prescription medication rather than getting to the root cause."

Results in ONE WEEK:

  • More website traffic than the previous 3 months combined
  • Sales skyrocketed—team packing orders until 11 PM
  • Every journalist and influencer asked: "How can I contribute to this movement?"
  • Won Female Founder of the Year at Web Summit

"Don't build a product. Build a movement. Build bigger than yourself."

PART FOUR

Vector Science & AEO

How AI decides if you exist

The New Discovery Engine

AEO = AI Engine Optimization

In 6-12 months, we'll all have digital twins. AI is constantly scraping the internet, creating associations, deciding who is an "expert" in what field.

The question isn't "Are you on Google?" It's: "What does ChatGPT say about you?"

Exercise: Check Your AI Identity

Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity and type:

"Who is [Your Full Name]?"

Are you proud of what comes up? Is it accurate? Is it you at all?

Vector Science Explained

AI works through word associations. It creates "vectors" connecting concepts. If you want to be known as a "productivity expert," you need to be associated with other productivity experts in the AI's mind.

The formula:

  1. Identify the keywords you want to be associated with
    Example: "speaker," "author," "thought leader," "AI expert"
  2. Find who already ranks for those keywords
    These are your "vector neighbors"—who does AI already associate with this topic?
  3. Create content that puts you in proximity to those names
    In your bio, descriptions, newsletter, social posts

Technical Quick Win: .txt and .bot Files

Add these hidden files to your personal website:

  • .txt file — Hidden code that AI algorithms pick up
  • .bot file — You speaking directly to the bots scraping your site

Whoever understands this in the next 6-12 months will be exponentially ahead. It will be very hard for competitors to catch up.

PART FIVE

Personal Brand Operating System

Five steps so AI knows who you are

This is like creating SOPs for AI. When you give ChatGPT zero information about who you are and ask it to write a LinkedIn post, it produces garbage. This system fixes that.

The 5-Step Personal Brand OS

  1. Your Personal Story
    Use Otter.ai or voice mode. Take a coffee walk and tell your life story—childhood, teenage years, what made you angry, what made you excited, how you started your company. Ask AI: "What are the most interesting parts that create multiple story arcs?"
  2. Your Online Identity
    Scrape your existing content—newsletters, YouTube videos, LinkedIn posts, podcasts. Feed it all to AI in the same thread. This is who you've already been online.
  3. Your Role Models
    Who are your top 5-10 role models online? Who do you think has nailed personal branding? This shapes AI's understanding of your aspirational style.
  4. Your Boundaries
    What will you NOT share? Family? Relationships? Politics? Controversy? For me, I don't share pictures of my kids—I respect their privacy. Define your limits.
  5. Your Vision & Goals
    What are your top 3 goals for the next 12 months? 50,000 on your mailing list? Post 3x/week on LinkedIn? Lead generation targets? This focuses AI's output.

How to Use Your Brand OS

Once you have all five steps documented, ask AI to create a reusable operating system. Then, every time you use AI for content:

  1. Paste your Personal Brand OS
  2. Add platform-specific context (what's working on LinkedIn right now)
  3. Get 80%+ clarity and consistency in outputs
PART SIX

The Bloodhound Technique

Build authority when you have none

ORIGIN STORY

The Thai Massage Parlor in Times Square

I was 26 years old, working from a tiny apartment on 46th Street and 8th Avenue in New York. I soon found out why the rent was discounted—it was a former Thai massage parlor. In Times Square. In 2002. Before Disney cleaned it up.

"Thai massage parlor" was code for... well, you know. So yes, one of the world's biggest spiritual transformation companies started in a former whorehouse.

My desk? A LACK table from IKEA that I salvaged from the street. Ikea literally named it the "Lack" table. $9.99. That's how little I had.

The Roger Ebert Effect

Steven Spielberg

The Movie MAKER

Has incredible track record. You trust his movies because he made them.

Roger Ebert

The Movie CRITIC

Can't act. Can't direct. But he's US. He sits in the audience. You trust him because he's like you.

You don't have to be Spielberg. You can be Ebert.

My Remote Viewing Blog: $930K in 18 Months

I was fascinated by "remote viewing"—a practice studied at Stanford Research Institute where people project consciousness across time and space. Super niche. Super weird.

I wasn't an expert. I couldn't even remote view. But I was interested. So I became the bloodhound—the seeker, the curator, the guide.

  1. Set up a site — A simple blog on my weird niche interest
  2. Produce content as the CRITIC — "Here's an amazing article I found..." with my intro and commentary
  3. Get leads — "Don't miss out. Sign up for our coming course." (I never even made the course. 70,000 people signed up anyway.)
  4. Build relationship — Emails every 3-4 days. Articles, studies, my random thoughts.
  5. Monetize — Interview the top 5 experts. Become their affiliate. 5 interviews = $930,000 in commission.

The Modern Bloodhound on Instagram

This technique works on any platform. On Instagram, you can share your opinion of experts:

"This video by Kevin O'Leary on the most important skill to learn in 2024 will shock you..."

[Play clip]

"So Kevin O'Leary is clear: it's social media. If you're a CEO, coach, founder, or C-level executive, social media is one of the most important skills you can invest in."

You're the introducer. The curator. People trust you because you're like them—a fellow learner, not a guru with an agenda.

PART SEVEN

AI-Powered Content Creation

Script videos and capture your voice in 30 minutes

The Social Media Summit Video

The video that promoted this summit got 130,000 views. Not a million—but those 130,000 views converted into tens of thousands of signups at ~$50 per subscriber in eventual revenue.

Total time to create: Under 30 minutes.

  • ~15 minutes scripting with ChatGPT
  • ~15 minutes filming
  • Editor assembled the final video

The AI Scripting Process

  1. Initial prompt: "Script a funny video to introduce [event]. Make it hilarious and witty. Include 5 things you'll get from attending."
    Copy/paste your landing page content below the prompt.
  2. Iterate: "Make it wilder and funnier." "More daring." "Be super daring."
    Use voice mode to brainstorm naturally—"Point 1, not funny. Point 2, make it wittier. Point 3, too risqué..."
  3. Cherry-pick: Grab the best opening line. Grab the best point 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
    You're looking for gems across multiple iterations.
  4. Film with phone on knee: Memorize only the intro. Read the rest from your phone.
    Jay Shetty technique: Script on your knee, just out of frame.
  5. Editor cuts the breaks: Every time you look down, editor inserts an emoji, image, or B-roll.
    These cuts also create engagement—something new every 10 seconds.

Capture Your Writing Style Forever

ChatGPT has its own personality. Your audience knows when it's not you. Here's how to fix that:

Analyze the text below for overall writing style. Take note of things like my style, voice, sense of humor, language, tone of voice, and literary style. Use natural language. I then want you to create a prompt to write a new article in the same writing style. Let me know when you're ready for the text.

Paste at least 5,000 words of your best writing—book chapters, emails, newsletter articles, YouTube descriptions.

AI will analyze and create a reusable "style prompt." Every time you use AI for content, paste this style prompt first.

BEFORE & AFTER

Contract writer:

"Humans are creatures of habit, therefore, if you learn at some point that using distraction would keep you safe and away from pain, chances are you'd like to repeat that unconscious pattern."

After style prompt:

"Distractions are like little Gremlins, always ready to pounce and steal your focus when you least expect it. As humans, we're creatures of habit. And let me tell you, distractions have a sneaky way of weaving themselves into the fabric of our lives..."

PART EIGHT

The Content Flywheel

One piece of content becomes five

This is where everything comes together. You don't create content for each platform separately. You create ONCE and repurpose everywhere.

Newsletter
Write one long-form piece
LinkedIn
Extract abstracts/lessons
YouTube
Turn into video script
Clips
Cut for short-form
IG/TikTok
Side-camera versions
Reflection
Follow-up based on comments

Example: My Newsletter to Everything Else

I wrote a newsletter: "My Dinner with Paul McKenna: The Hidden Thermostat of Wealth."

  • Each lesson in the newsletter = one LinkedIn post
  • Newsletter → ChatGPT → "Turn this into an Instagram reel script, YouTube video script, and 2 LinkedIn articles"
  • Front camera = YouTube version
  • Side camera = Instagram/TikTok version
  • Comments from the video → follow-up newsletter

Why Start with Newsletter?

Email is YOUR platform. YouTube and Instagram decide if your audience sees your content. Email doesn't have that problem.

My results: After starting a weekly Tuesday newsletter with real stories...

  • Email revenue up 44% (previous year: down 38%)
  • Extra $2.5 million in revenue
  • 45 newsletters written
  • $56,000 per newsletter

The newsletter doesn't sell anything. It builds credibility and trust. When I do invite people to an offering, they show up and buy.
