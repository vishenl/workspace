December 2024 — What Changed

2:30 - 3:30
Story Setup

This is the context for your bold claim. Make the copywriter comparison vivid — they remember when that happened. Then pivot to programmers.

SLIDE: AI did to programmers what it did to copywriters

Two years ago, AI did something to copywriters. PAUSE

It made them essentially optional.

Suddenly, anyone could create copy in any language. Instantly. The entire profession got disrupted overnight.

BEAT

In December 2024, that same thing happened to programmers.

The new version of Claude came out. And it proved that it could code incredibly well.

GRAVITY Before December, programmers had to check AI code. After December? They don't.

By the end of December, Stack Overflow — the place programmers go to ask questions — had reached its lowest level of activity ever.

PAUSE

This isn't speculation. This is what just happened.

The Hidden Truth — What Headlines Miss

6:00 - 7:00
Data Reframe

Counter the fear narrative. Headlines say "jobs lost" — you're showing the fuller picture. The 56% should feel like relief.

SLIDE: 32% vs 56%

Here's what headlines miss.

When McKinsey asked leaders what they expect to happen to their workforce:

32% expect decreases. That's what makes the news.

BEAT

But 43% expect no change. And 13% actually expect increases.

That's HOPE 56% of leaders who see AI as augmentation — not replacement.

BEAT

The World Economic Forum found similar patterns. 78% of existing jobs will be enhanced — not eliminated.

And 24% expect entirely new jobs to emerge that don't even exist yet.

Sam Altman's 5 Levels of AI

7:00 - 8:30
Framework

This gives them a mental map. The key revelation: we're at Level 3 and it took 2 years, not a decade. Let that sink in.

SLIDE: 5 Levels

Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI, describes five levels of AI development.

BEAT

Level 1: Chatbots. AI that can have conversations. We've completed this.

Level 2: Reasoners. AI that can think through complex problems. We've completed this.

Level 3: Agents. AI that takes action. Browses the web. Books appointments. Executes multi-step tasks. WEIGHT We're here right now.

PAUSE

Level 4: Innovators. AI that generates genuinely novel ideas.

Level 5: One-Person Billion-Dollar Companies. AI so capable that a single person can build what once required thousands.

BEAT

URGENCY The journey from Level 1 to Level 3 took two years.

Experts predicted it would take a decade.

Five Human Superpowers

11:30 - 13:00
Delivery Note

Count these with your fingers. Each one is a gift. Let them feel empowered — these are THEIR competitive advantages.

SLIDE: 5 Human Superpowers

Five human superpowers that AI cannot replicate — and that will become more valuable, not less.

BEAT

Number One: Emotional Resonance.

Making another human being feel truly seen. AI can simulate empathy. It cannot be empathetic.

BEAT

Number Two: Moral Imagination.

Navigating ethical complexity. When there's no clear right answer — you need human judgment.

BEAT

Number Three: Creative Intuition.

AI can remix the past. Truly new ideas come from human dreams.

BEAT

Number Four: Embodied Presence.

Being fully here. When someone walks into a room and changes the energy — that's human.

BEAT

Number Five: Meaning-Making.

Transforming data into purpose. Facts into vision. Only you can decide what they mean.

The Centaur Model

13:00 - 14:00
SLIDE: Human + AI > AI alone

In chess, who wins? The best human grandmaster? Or the best supercomputer?

BEAT

Neither.

The most powerful chess players are centaurs — humans working with machines.

A grandmaster alone loses to a supercomputer.

But a grandmaster with a laptop? Beats the supercomputer.

BEAT

EMPOWERMENT Human + AI is greater than AI alone.

The future doesn't belong to AI.

The future belongs to centaurs.

14

Five Moves That Matter

14:00 - 15:00
Delivery Note

Shift to practical mode. These are actionable. Speak directly to the leaders — these are things they can DO tomorrow.

SLIDE: Five Moves

So what do you do with all of this? Five moves that separate AI leaders from AI laggards.

BEAT

One: Redesign workflows from scratch.

Don't bolt AI onto old processes. Ask: "If we started today, how would we do this?"

Two: Invest in human skills.

Emotional intelligence. Creative problem-solving. Judgment. These become your edge.

Three: Model the behavior as leaders.

If you're not using AI tools daily, you're signaling it's not important.

Four: Aim for transformation, not just efficiency.

Efficiency is the floor. Innovation is the ceiling.

Five: Train everyone.

AI literacy should be as universal as email literacy.
