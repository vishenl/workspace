The Secret The Ancient Stoics Knew About Becoming Unstoppable

And why most people get goal-setting completely backwards

Vishen Lakhiani

December 2024

A few years ago, I hit rock bottom.

Not financially. Mindvalley was doing well. Not in my relationships. My family was healthy and happy.

It was something harder to explain.

I was reactive. I'd wake up and immediately feel the weight of a thousand decisions pressing down on me. Every email felt urgent. Every problem felt like a crisis. I was constantly putting out fires, and the fires never stopped.

I was successful on the outside. But on the inside? I was exhausted, scattered, and one bad day away from completely losing it.

Sound familiar?

Here's what I've learned since then — something I wish someone had told me twenty years ago:

"Your goals will only match your frequency."

Let me explain what I mean.

The Backwards Approach to Goal-Setting

Every January, billions of people around the world sit down to set their goals for the new year.

They write down what they want to achieve. They make vision boards. They set SMART goals. They buy planners and apps and accountability partners.

And by February? Most of them have already given up.

We blame ourselves. We think we lack willpower, discipline, or commitment. We think maybe we just don't want it enough.

But that's not the problem.

The problem is that we're trying to create a new life from the same old state.

Think about it: when you sit down to set goals, what state are you usually in? Exhausted from the holidays. Confused about what you really want. Anxious about whether you can actually pull it off. Still carrying emotional baggage from the year before.

And from THAT place — you try to design your dream life?

If you plan from chaos, you create chaos.

If you plan from scarcity, you create scarcity.

If you plan from anxiety, you create more things to be anxious about.

This is what the ancient philosophers understood — and what modern psychology is now confirming.

What The Stoics Knew

Marcus Aurelius was the most powerful man in the ancient world. As Roman Emperor, he faced wars, plagues, betrayals, and the constant threat of assassination.

And yet, he remained calm. Grounded. Unshakeable.

How?

The Stoics had a word for it: Ataraxia.

It translates roughly to "a state of serene calmness" — but it's more than that. It's a state of being so centered, so rooted in who you are, that external circumstances simply cannot disturb your inner peace.

They didn't achieve this through positive thinking or affirmations. They achieved it through practice. Through deliberately cultivating certain internal states until those states became their default way of being.

I call these the 7 Sacred States.

The 7 Sacred States

For the past several years, I've been studying what makes certain people unshakeable. I've interviewed over a thousand of the world's top performers, spiritual teachers, and scientists. I've dug into the research. I've tested practices on myself and with thousands of Mindvalley students.

What I found is that there are seven specific internal states that, when cultivated, make you what I call "unfuckwithable."

Yes, I know that's a strong word. But there's no better way to describe it.

Unfuckwithable means you're truly at peace and in touch with yourself. Nothing anyone says or does bothers you, and no negativity can touch you. You're grounded in who you are. Clear on what matters. Unattached to outcomes but fully committed to your path.

Here's a glimpse of what these states look like:

Knowingness

The state of understanding who you are at the deepest level. Getting clear on your values. Standing in your worth with unshakeable certainty. When you embody knowingness, you stop seeking external validation — because you already know.

Faith

Not religious faith (though it can include that). This is the ability to trust before you have proof. To move forward confidently even when you can't see the full path. What's meant for you cannot miss you. What misses you was never yours.

Non-Attachment

Wanting something, working for it with everything you have — but not needing it. Steve Jobs got fired from Apple. He shrugged, started Pixar, and eventually returned to build the most valuable company on Earth. That's non-attachment in action.

There are four more states — each equally powerful, each with specific practices to cultivate them.

But here's what matters most:

When you operate from these states, everything changes.

Your decisions become clearer. Your relationships deepen. Opportunities that used to pass you by suddenly seem to find you. Problems that used to derail you for days now roll off your back.

You stop reacting to life. You start creating it.

Why This Matters Right Now

We're entering a new year. And if you're like most people, you're probably already thinking about what you want to achieve, change, or become in 2025.

But here's my invitation:

What if, instead of setting goals first, you reset your inner state first?

What if you entered January 1st not from exhaustion and anxiety, but from deep knowingness, unshakeable faith, and radical openness to receive?

What kind of goals would you set THEN?

What kind of year would you create?

Here's what I know for certain:

The goals you set from your highest state will be completely different from the goals you set from burnout. They'll be bigger. Clearer. More aligned with who you really are.

And — this is the counterintuitive part — they'll actually be easier to achieve. Because you won't be fighting yourself the whole way.
