YouTube Positioning Strategy

The Reframe: From "Happiness" to "Flawesome"

The original Quest is called "7 Days to Happiness" - a solid concept but generic in the crowded YouTube wellness space. For YouTube, we reposition this as "7 Days to Flawesome" - leveraging Kristina's unique, ownable concept that differentiates her from every other happiness guru.

This isn't about chasing happiness. It's about accepting yourself - flaws and all - and discovering that's where true happiness actually lives.

"7 Days to stop fixing yourself and start accepting yourself"

🎪 The Hook

Pattern Interrupt: While everyone teaches "how to be happier," Kristina teaches "why trying to be happier is making you miserable."

The counterintuitive angle: Stop chasing happiness. Embrace your flaws. Happiness follows.

👤 Target Audience

High-achieving perfectionists (28-55, primarily female) who:

  • Have tried other happiness/self-help content
  • Feel exhausted by "positive thinking"
  • Want permission to be imperfect
  • Are ready for a different approach

🔥 Viral Potential

Challenge Format: Each video ends with a specific daily challenge viewers can do and share.

  • Hashtag: #7DaysToFlawesome
  • Community engagement in comments
  • Shareable "confession" style content
  • Day-by-day progression builds habit

📊 Content Structure

Video Length: 8-12 minutes each (optimal for YouTube algorithm)

  • Strong hook (first 30 seconds)
  • Personal story from Kristina
  • Core teaching (3-5 minutes)
  • Daily challenge assignment
  • CTA to watch next day

Key Differentiators for YouTube

  • Anti-Perfectionism Angle: Contrarian to most self-help content ("stop trying so hard")
  • Flawesome Branding: Unique, memorable, searchable term Kristina owns
  • Real Vulnerability: Kristina shares genuine struggles, not polished platitudes
  • Challenge Format: Creates daily engagement and return viewers
  • Philosophical Depth: Appeals to intelligent audience seeking substance

7-Day Challenge Overview

1

The Present Moment Practice

Original Quest Topic: Being Present
"Why Your Brain Is Stealing Your Happiness (And the 60-Second Fix)"

Core Concept: Happiness only exists in the present moment. When we feel negative emotions, we're usually mentally time-traveling to the past (regret, anger) or future (fear, worry). The perfectionist's mind is especially prone to this - always reviewing past "failures" or worrying about future "mistakes."

What Kristina Will Cover

  • Personal story: Singing lullabies while mentally planning tomorrow's video
  • The emotion time-map: Where negative emotions actually live
  • Why perfectionists are the worst at being present
  • The 60-second reset technique when you catch yourself time-traveling

Daily Challenge

"Today, catch yourself 3 times when your mind leaves the present. Notice: is this thought about the past or future? Then take 60 seconds to anchor back. Share one moment you caught yourself in the comments."

2

The Comparison Trap

Original Quest Topic: No Comparison Day
"The Silicon Valley Suicide Story: Why Comparing Yourself to Others Is Literally Killing You"

Core Concept: Comparison is the perfectionist's drug of choice. We compare our insides to other people's outsides, our chapter 1 to their chapter 20. The story of a successful entrepreneur who became suicidal despite having "everything" - because someone always had more.

What Kristina Will Cover

  • True story of a Silicon Valley friend who nearly died from comparison
  • The apples-to-oranges fallacy: comparing business results to self-worth
  • The difference between inspiring comparison and debilitating comparison
  • Why "No Comparison Day" is revolutionary for perfectionists

Daily Challenge

"Today is your No Comparison Day. Every time you catch yourself comparing, say 'STOP' out loud. At the end of the day, comment how many times you caught yourself. (My first time was 47 times.)"

3

Gratitude That Actually Works

Original Quest Topic: Gratitude Practice
"Why Most Gratitude Practices Are BS (And the Science of What Actually Works)"

Core Concept: Gratitude isn't about putting on rose-tinted glasses or pretending bad things are good. The University of California research shows 25% increase in happiness from proper gratitude practice. The secret is specificity, emotion, and the three categories.

What Kristina Will Cover

  • Why "just be grateful" advice is useless (and what to do instead)
  • The three categories: Personal life, Work life, and Self
  • Why gratitude for yourself is the hardest (and most important)
  • The science: How to actually feel gratitude, not just list it

Daily Challenge

"Write 5 things in each category (personal, work, self). But here's the twist: for the 'self' category, include something about your flaws. Comment your hardest one to write."

4

The Nuclear Explosion Exercise

Original Quest Topic: Love & Relationships
"If You Had 15 Minutes Before the World Ended, Who Would You Call?"

Core Concept: The Harvard 75-year study proves relationships are the #1 factor in happiness and longevity. Lisa Nichols' "Nuclear Explosion" exercise reveals what we're not saying to the people who matter most. Why wait for the apocalypse?

What Kristina Will Cover

  • Why Kristina resisted this category (and why she was wrong)
  • The 75-year Harvard study on what actually makes us happy
  • Lisa Nichols' Nuclear Explosion exercise that changed everything
  • What perfectionists struggle to say to people they love

Daily Challenge

"List 5 people. Write what you'd tell them if the world ended in 15 minutes. Then actually contact ONE of them today. Share in the comments: Who did you reach out to? (No names needed - just 'my mom' or 'my best friend')"

5

Permission to Be Imperfect

Original Quest Topic: Perfectionism
"The Perfect Desk Story: Why Your 'Perfect Picture' Is Making You Miserable"

Core Concept: The story of the scientist whose daughter rearranged his desk - and why having a "perfect picture" of how life should be guarantees unhappiness. When you're happy, everything IS perfect. Not the other way around.

What Kristina Will Cover

  • Personal confession: "I'm a recovering perfectionist"
  • The desk story: How perfect pictures create guaranteed disappointment
  • The flawsome concept: Flaws + Awesome = Your superpower
  • Lisa Nichols quote: "The world doesn't need your perfection"

Daily Challenge

"Today, intentionally do ONE thing imperfectly. Post something unedited. Send an email with a typo. Leave one dish in the sink. Comment what you did imperfectly - and how it felt."

6

The Forgiveness Practice

Original Quest Topic: Forgiveness
"Drinking Poison and Hoping They'll Die: The Real Truth About Forgiveness"

Core Concept: The Buddha quote says it all: "Holding onto resentment is like drinking poison hoping the offender will suffer." Forgiveness isn't about the other person - it's about emptying your backpack of rotten potatoes. The parable that changes everything.

What Kristina Will Cover

  • Why forgiveness is the hardest practice (and most transformative)
  • The backpack of potatoes parable
  • "Hurt people hurt people" - the movie "Wonder" and perspective shifts
  • Forgiveness doesn't mean forgetting or allowing - what it actually means

Daily Challenge

"Think of someone you're holding resentment toward. Not your biggest enemy - start with someone you actually WANT to forgive. Write them a letter you'll never send. Comment: one word for how you feel after writing it."

7

Making Friends With Pain

Original Quest Topic: Dealing with Pain
"Stop Running From Pain: Why Your Negative Emotions Are Actually Trying to Help You"

Core Concept: The personal growth world has a dirty secret: we pretend we can eliminate negative emotions. The books "F*ck Emotions" and "Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck" reveal our cultural denial. Pain is given; suffering is a choice. Here's how to work WITH emotions, not against them.

What Kristina Will Cover

  • Why trying to be Zen all the time is actually toxic
  • Brene Brown: "Children are hardwired for suffering"
  • The 4-step technique: Name, Allow, Locate, Release
  • Why the gym metaphor reframes everything about emotional pain

Daily Challenge

"The next time you feel something uncomfortable today, try the 4-step process: Name it precisely, Allow it to exist, Locate it in your body, Relax that body part. Comment: What did you name your emotion? (Get specific - not just 'sad' but 'disappointed in myself')"
