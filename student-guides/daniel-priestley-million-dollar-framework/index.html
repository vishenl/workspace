Step 1 - Below The Line

The Entrepreneur Apprenticeship

Before you launch a business, you need to prepare yourself. The apprenticeship phase is about gaining the three essential foundations every entrepreneur needs.

1

Commercial Awareness

Understand how businesses work - how they make sales, look after customers, and generate revenue.

2

Self-Awareness

Know what you're good at, your strengths, your weaknesses, and how you tick as a person.

3

Access to Resources

Have some money saved, know how to run ads, hire salespeople, or access capital when needed.

How to Know You're Ready

  • When someone asks "Do you know how businesses make sales?" you say "Yes, I've seen that plenty of times."
  • You can explain what you're strong at and what your weaknesses are.
  • You understand how to access resources - money, ads, hiring, etc.
  • You've worked around an entrepreneur for 6 months to 2 years.
Step 2 - Below The Line

The 90-Day Side Hustle

Test your entrepreneurial skills with time-boxed experiments. The key: build them so they END at 90 days. It's not about making money - it's about building confidence and experience.

Side Hustle Examples

Pop-up Agency Client

Work for a client as a side hustle in the area you want to pursue as an entrepreneur.

Event Organizing

Daniel ran nightclub parties as side hustles to test marketing and sales skills.

Training/Consulting

Deliver sales training or consulting in a focused 90-day engagement.

Key Principle

"The most important thing is that you build it so that it ends at 90 days. It's not about making money. It's about getting yourself ready to be an entrepreneur."

- Daniel Priestley

Step 3 - Above The Line

The CAOS Method

Think of yourself as a scientist running fast, cheap experiments. Test these four elements to find product-market fit.

C

Concept

Is this a good concept? Does it solve a real problem people care about?

A

Audience

Have you found the right people who value this and are willing to pay?

O

Offer

Can you offer them what they want at a price they're happy to pay?

S

Sales

Are you good at selling this? Can you close deals consistently?

MVPs (Minimum Viable Products)

Test things in the market as though they're real, even though they're not. Most MVPs are landing pages.

Waiting List

Ask people to join a waiting list for something coming in the future.

Event Registration

Zoom event, live event, dinner party, or launch event.

Trial Signup

Get people to sign up for a trial or early access.

5 Questions for Your Waiting List

  1. What type of person are you? (Student, Executive, Entrepreneur, Parent)
  2. What results are you trying to achieve?
  3. What's your biggest challenge or frustration?
  4. What else have you tried?
  5. What's your current budget for solving this?

The Magic Number: 30-150 Sales Meetings

You're not looking to make sales. You're looking to improve your product-market fit. When customers say no, don't get deflated - get excited and ask for free consulting.

Step 4

Building a 6-Figure Business

Now you're ramping up revenue, building a team, and creating a repeatable system that generates $50K-$100K per month.

The 4-Person Team

Associate KPI

Someone with a bigger brand who lends credibility to your business (advisor, board member).

Sales & Marketing

Hopefully this is YOU. Drive all sales and marketing activities.

Customer Success

Handles delivery and ensures customers achieve results.

Operations

Social media, marketing assistant, keeps the engine running.

Two Product Types

Product for Prospects

Low-commitment, easy first step. This is what you PROMOTE (not your main offering).

  • Introduction workshop
  • Assessment
  • Free consultation
Core Offer

Your main revenue generator. Ideally in Gold, Silver, Bronze tiers.

  • Slide deck or brochure
  • Clear pricing tiers
  • Presented in 1-to-1 meetings

The Perfect Repeatable Week

Design a week that, when repeated, generates $50K-$100K per month. Include:

Lead Generation
  • Ads (with allowable cost per sale)
  • Social posting (daily, 3+ platforms)
  • 100 DMs/emails per day
  • Join social media groups
Sales & Operations
  • 1-to-1 selling
  • Group presentations (1-to-10)
  • Monday 3-6 things meeting
  • Friday afternoon debrief
