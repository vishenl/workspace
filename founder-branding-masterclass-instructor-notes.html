The Visibility Wound

7 min Slides 3-9
The "Hey Hayden" Story

"In 2015, I stood outside Steve Jobs' house in Silicon Valley. I'd been running Mindvalley for 10 years—$30-40 million in revenue, no venture capital. A successful founder by any measure."

"But I couldn't bring myself to share on social media. Who the fuck cares about me? My authors were famous. I was just the guy behind the curtain."

"So I came up with a hack. I recorded videos for an audience of one: my six-year-old son Hayden."

"Hey Hayden, check it out..."
"Because if I was just talking to my kid, nobody could tell me I'm an idiot."
Pause. Let the absurdity sink in. Self-deprecating smile.
"The funny thing?"
"My son was six. He didn't really give a fuck."
Beat. Shrug. "But nobody could call me out, because I was just being a good dad."
"I ended every video with 'Miss you Hayden. See you soon.' That way, nobody could judge me. It was armor disguised as affection."
The Visibility Wound — A fear of being seen. Shockingly common among founders. My newsletter article "Do You Have a Visibility Wound?" got more reads than anything I'd written in six months.
The Jay Shetty Conversation

"In 2017, at a Mindvalley event in Sardinia, I was interviewing Jay Shetty. And I told him: 'Jay, I'm 44 years old. I'm too old for this. I don't have your hair. I don't know how to dress like you.'"

"Jay said..."
"'V, it's all bullshit excuses. You're fine. Just start.'"
Do a casual "Jay Shetty" voice if you can pull it off. Warm, no-nonsense.
"That week, I posted my first real Instagram post. A joke about a bad haircut. And my dad thought I joined a football league."
SLIDE 8 "From 30,000 followers, I grew to 1.1 million. And here's the crazy part: my personal Instagram gets 3x more engagement than the Mindvalley company account."
The world has shifted. We don't want to follow companies anymore—we want to follow individuals. As a founder, YOU are your company's greatest marketing weapon.
"Now, let me tell you about a woman who took this to the extreme..."

The New Founder Era

4 min Slides 10-13
SLIDE 10 "Founders no longer just build companies. They build movements. People buy what you stand for, not just what you sell."
SLIDE 11 "Don't build a product. Build a movement. Build bigger than yourself."
The Nora Cavani Story

"Nora Cavani, founder of Alba Health—she tests children's gut microbiomes. You basically swab your kid's poop, send it in, they tell you what's wrong."

"Yes, there's gold in them hills."
"Literal gold... in poop."
Deadpan. Let them groan. Move on quickly.
"She was about to pitch at a London tech event. Supposed to talk about 'technology' and 'testing.' But fifteen minutes before going on stage, she remembered something from Mindvalley: Build bigger than yourself."
"Instead of pitching technology, she spoke about a movement: 'I'm building a movement around healthy guts for kids. Your child may not be sick—their microbiome is sick.'"
SLIDE 13 "Results in ONE WEEK: More website traffic than the previous three months combined. Sales skyrocketed—team packing orders until 11 PM. Every journalist asked: 'How can I contribute?' She won Female Founder of the Year at Web Summit."
"Now let's talk about WHERE to build your movement..."

Founder Media Ecosystem

6 min Slides 14-17
SLIDE 14 "This is one of the most important frameworks I learned from my advisor Sabrina Stocker. Each platform is a different room—you speak differently in each."
SLIDE 15 "YouTube is the Classroom—educate and inspire. Direct to camera. Like having coffee with someone, teaching them across the table."
"LinkedIn is the Boardroom—lead and influence. Text beats video. Vulnerability wins. Share your process, not your polish."
"Instagram is the Cafe—connect and entertain. Side camera. They're observing you being interviewed. This is where you go viral."
"Newsletter is the Sanctuary—nurture and deepen trust. This is YOUR platform. No algorithm throttling. Your list, your rules."
The Two-Camera Technique — Record once, get content for two platforms. Front camera for YouTube. Side camera for Instagram/TikTok.
"So you set up two cameras..."
"Not lazy. Leverage."
Wink. "Work smarter, not harder."
"Which platform will you focus on? Put it in the chat. I want to see where you're putting your energy."
"Which platform do you choose?"
"The one you'll actually use. Consistency beats optimization."
Pause. "I don't care what the algorithm says. I care what you'll actually do."
"Now let's talk about something that will blow your mind..."

Vector Science & AEO

5 min Slides 18-21
SLIDE 18 "AEO. AI Engine Optimization. In 6-12 months, we'll all have digital twins. AI is constantly scraping the internet, creating associations, deciding who is an 'expert' in what."
SLIDE 19 "The question isn't 'Are you on Google?' It's: 'What does ChatGPT say about you?'"
"I Googled myself recently..."
"Google is giving FAQs about me that are completely wrong. Apparently people want to know some deeply personal stuff. And the AI has just... made it up."
Genuine bewilderment. "At least it knew I existed."
"Right now, open ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity. Type: 'Who is [your name]?' Are you proud of what comes up?"
Vector Science — AI works through word associations. If you want to be known as a "productivity expert," you need to be associated with other productivity experts in the AI's mind.
SLIDE 21 "Three steps: 1) Identify keywords you want to be associated with. 2) Find who already ranks. 3) Create content that puts you in proximity to those names."
"Quick technical win: Add .txt and .bot files to your website. The bots are scraping. Speak to them directly."
"Now let me show you how to train AI to actually understand YOU..."

Personal Brand Operating System

4 min Slides 22-23
SLIDE 22 "This is like creating SOPs for AI. When you give ChatGPT zero information about who you are and ask it to write a LinkedIn post... garbage."
"Have you ever asked AI to write content for you?"
"And it sounds like a motivational poster had a baby with a corporate memo?"
Mimick bland AI voice: "In today's fast-paced world..." Eye roll.
SLIDE 23 "Five steps to your Personal Brand OS:"
"1. Your Personal Story — Take a coffee walk. Use Otter.ai or voice mode. Tell AI your life from birth to now. Ask it: 'What are the most interesting parts that create story arcs?'"
"2. Your Online Identity — Feed it your existing content. Newsletters, videos, LinkedIn posts. This is who you've already been."
"3. Your Role Models — Who has nailed personal branding? Whose style do you admire?"
"4. Your Boundaries..."
"For me, I don't share pictures of my kids. I once threw my laptop across the room when someone tried to put my kids' photos in a presentation."
Slight exaggeration smile. "Okay, maybe not threw. But I wanted to."
"5. Your Vision & Goals — Top 3 goals for the next 12 months. This focuses AI's output."
"Now let me share my most controversial technique..."

The Bloodhound Technique

8 min Slides 24-28
The Thai Massage Parlor Origin Story

"I was 26. Working from a tiny apartment on 46th Street and 8th Avenue in New York. I soon found out why the rent was discounted—it was a former Thai massage parlor. In Times Square. In 2002. Before Disney cleaned it up."

"Thai massage parlor is code for..."
"Well, you know. So yes, one of the world's biggest spiritual transformation companies started in a former whorehouse in Times Square."
Long pause. Let them process. "Start sucky, remember?"
"My desk was a LACK table from IKEA."
"Ikea LITERALLY named it 'Lack.' As in, you lack money if you're buying this. Nine dollars and ninety-nine cents."
Show the photo if you have it. "That's how little I had."
The Roger Ebert Effect — You can be Steven Spielberg (the maker) OR Roger Ebert (the critic). Both have authority. One makes movies. One watches them and tells us what's good.
"Roger Ebert can't act. Can't direct. But he's US. He sits in the audience. You trust him because he's like you."
SLIDE 26 "You don't have to be Spielberg. You can be Ebert."
The Remote Viewing Blog

"I was fascinated by 'remote viewing'—a practice studied at Stanford where people project consciousness across time and space. George Clooney made a movie about it: Men Who Stared at Goats. Super niche. Super weird."

"I wasn't an expert. I couldn't even remote view. But I was INTERESTED. So I became the bloodhound—the seeker, the curator, the guide."

"I searched the internet for the best articles, the best studies. I'd write: 'Hey, here's an amazing article I found...' and give my commentary. I was the critic, not the creator."
"I added a box: 'Sign up for our coming course!'"
"I never made the course. 70,000 people signed up anyway."
Let that sink in. "Seventy. Thousand. For a course that didn't exist."
SLIDE 27 "Five interviews with experts. Became their affiliate. $930,000 in commission in 18 months. From a niche I had zero expertise in."
The Bloodhound Model: 1) Set up site 2) Content as critic 3) Get leads 4) Build relationship 5) Monetize through affiliates/interviews
"Now let me show you how to create content in 30 minutes..."

AI-Powered Content Creation

6 min Slides 29-32
SLIDE 30 "The video that promoted this summit got 130,000 views. Total time to create: under 30 minutes. About 15 minutes scripting with ChatGPT, 15 minutes filming."
"I prompted ChatGPT: 'Make it funny.'"
"It wasn't funny. So I said: 'Wilder and funnier.' Still not funny. 'MORE daring.' Finally got something."
Mime typing aggressively. "Sometimes you have to bully the AI."
"I use voice mode. 'Point 1, not funny. Point 2, make it wittier. Point 3, too risqué—don't go there.' I'm having a conversation with my creative director."
"Then I cherry-pick. Best opening from iteration 3. Best point 1 from iteration 7. Best closing from iteration 2. Frankenstein it together."
The Jay Shetty Technique — Script on your phone, phone on your knee. Memorize only the intro. Read the rest. Editor cuts the breaks and inserts images/emojis.
SLIDE 32 "Now, capturing your voice. ChatGPT has its own personality. Your audience knows when it's not you."
"Use this prompt: 'Analyze the text below for writing style, voice, sense of humor, language, tone. Create a prompt to write in my style.' Feed it 5,000+ words of your best writing."
"Before the style prompt, contract writers produce..."
"'Humans are creatures of habit...' So bloody boring I wanted to fire everyone."
Deadpan reading of bad copy.
"After the style prompt..."
"'Distractions are like little Gremlins, always ready to pounce...' NOW it sounds like me."
Animated, like you're reading something fun.
"Now let's bring it all together..."

The Content Flywheel

5 min Slides 33-35
SLIDE 34 "This is where everything comes together. You don't create content for each platform separately. You create ONCE and repurpose everywhere."
"The flywheel: Newsletter → LinkedIn → YouTube → Clips → Instagram/TikTok → Reflection → Newsletter"
"Example: I wrote 'My Dinner with Paul McKenna.' Each lesson becomes a LinkedIn post. The whole thing becomes a YouTube script. Front camera for YouTube, side camera for Instagram. Clips for TikTok. Comments feed my next newsletter."
Why start with Newsletter? Email is YOUR platform. No algorithm throttling. Your list, your rules.
SLIDE 35 "After I started a weekly newsletter with real stories... email revenue up 44%. Previous year: down 38%. Extra $2.5 million in revenue. $56,000 per newsletter."
"The newsletter doesn't sell anything."
"It builds credibility. So when I DO invite people to something, they show up. And buy."
Lean in conspiratorially. "The money is in the trust."
"Finally, let's talk about who you're becoming..."

The Modern Founder Archetype

3 min Slides 36-40
SLIDE 37 "You're not just a founder anymore. You're four archetypes in one."
"The Teacher — Share wisdom freely. You don't have to be a master. Teach what you're learning in real time."
"The Philosopher — Question norms. Share your thinking process, not just conclusions."
"The Storyteller — Turn experience into narrative. Stories create connection that facts cannot."
"The Rebel — Challenge the brules. The bullshit rules. The world is changing fast—there are so many rules to break."
SLIDE 38 "People don't follow products. They follow energy. You don't have to shout louder—you just have to speak more authentically."
"If you have a visibility wound, I've been there. It reappears. It came back for me in March. Couldn't share my wins. Cost me hundreds of thousands."
"But you'll overcome it. Because truth creates resonance. Authenticity builds trust. And in the age of AI, trust is the only currency that matters."
SLIDE 39 "Your playbook: 1) Pick ONE platform—I recommend email. 2) Publish one long-form piece weekly. 3) Repurpose across the ecosystem. 4) Create your Personal Brand OS."
SLIDE 40 "Start this weekend. Create your first piece of content. You're not creating it on every platform—just one. The one you'll actually use."
Final energy: grounded, encouraging, confident. They CAN do this.
