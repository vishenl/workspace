Core Teaching Philosophy

✨
  • 🎬
    Inspire first, teach second. Start with "wow" moments - show what's possible before explaining how.
  • 🔓
    Demystify the technology. Most students are intimidated by "coding." Your job is to make it feel accessible.
  • 🔄
    Emphasize iteration. Vibe coding is conversational - it's okay to refine and improve.
  • 🏆
    Celebrate progress over perfection. A working prototype is a win, even if it's not polished.

You're not teaching students to become developers. You're giving entrepreneurs a superpower - the ability to build their ideas without waiting for technical resources.

— The Core Mindset

Student Outcomes

By the end of both classes, students should be able to:

  • Use the VIBE framework to structure their app requirements
  • Build functional web applications with user accounts and databases
  • Connect external APIs (like OpenAI, Stripe) to add advanced features
  • Deploy their apps to a live URL they can share

Examples to Show at the Start

Open with real examples of what vibe coding can create. Show these to inspire before teaching:

  • Class notes & slides - The very slides and notes you're using were built with vibe coding
  • Curriculum documents - Spiritual mastery curriculum built from interviews with teachers
  • Celebration pages - Speaking mastery graduation celebrating student wins
  • Video analysis tools - Apps that analyze YouTube videos and suggest improvements
  • Company websites - Real business websites built in hours, not weeks
  • Supplement website pages - Product pages built by scanning existing sites and interviews
  • Personal apps - NutriTrack (food tracking with AI analysis and PubMed research)
Opening line: "These were all built by people just like you, with zero coding experience. And I'm going to show you how."

Core Principles from Live Classes

Principle 1: Start with MVP (Minimal Viable Product)

Don't try to build the entire dream app. Start with something small.

The Lake Analogy: "If you see a lake in the forest, you don't just jump in. You dip your toe in first, you test the water. Is it cold? Is it warm? A minimal viable product is testing the water."
  • For a complex app idea, the MVP might just be a landing page that asks: "Are you interested? Leave an email."
  • If interested, "Tell me why." If not interested, "Tell me why not."
  • This validates the idea before investing time in building

Principle 2: User Stories vs. Features

Don't tell the AI what to build. Describe the problem you're trying to solve.

✗ Feature Request (Avoid)

"I want a map that shows cemetery locations with clickable pins that open a modal with headstone photos..."

✓ User Story (Better)

"I am a 4th generation person in my family, and I've inherited a tote full of black and white photos. I want to honor my family members who have passed..."

When you share user stories instead of features, you let the AI come up with even better solutions than you imagined.

Principle 3: ChatGPT is Your CEO, Lovable is Your CTO

Think of it as having two team members:

  • ChatGPT = CMO/CEO: Creative co-visioning, brainstorming, understanding customer needs, making the product likable
  • Lovable/Claude = CTO: Spits out hard facts and logic, executes the technical implementation
The Temperature Difference: "My ChatGPT calls me 'bro' and has a witty sense of humor. It also makes stuff up 5% of the time because it's designed to be hyper-creative. My Claude is like someone with Asperger's - it speaks in straight logic, doesn't empathize, but it's more accurate for building tools."

Principle 4: There Is No Fancy Prompt

"You just talk to it like you're talking to your sister or your brother."

  • Don't worry about perfect prompt engineering
  • The one trick: Ask it to "ask 5 clarifying questions" - then the AI will ask what it needs to know
  • Natural conversation beats complex prompts

Principle 5: You Are the Boss

With AI, you're in charge. You can be direct.

Don't be polite: Instead of "Can we make the design purple?" just say "Make it purple." You can yell at it, be demanding - the AI won't mind. (Some say it's good therapy to take out frustrations on AI!)

The 5-Step Vibe Coding Workflow

This is the repeatable process that works every time:

1

Brainstorm with ChatGPT

Use ChatGPT as your creative partner. Describe your idea in natural language.

2

Answer Clarifying Questions

ChatGPT asks 5 questions to understand your vision. Answer quickly and simply.

3

Get the Lovable Prompt

Ask ChatGPT to create the first prompt for Lovable.

4

Paste into Lovable

Copy the prompt and paste directly into Lovable. Watch it build!

5

Iterate & Repeat

Go back to ChatGPT for the next step. Repeat until your app is complete.

Detailed Step Instructions

Step 1: Brainstorm with ChatGPT

Use ChatGPT as your creative partner. Describe your idea in natural language.

"Hey ChatGPT, I'm here with my friend [Name], and we want to brainstorm an app idea. I want you to listen to the app they want to build, and help us develop the idea further by asking 5 clarifying questions."

Step 2: Answer Clarifying Questions

ChatGPT will ask 5 questions to understand your vision. Answer quickly and simply. Examples:

  • "Who is this for?" → "Families and historical societies"
  • "What problem does it solve?" → "It connects generations together"
  • "What does the first version need to do?" → "Show a map to locate headstones"
Keep it simple: Don't over-explain. When a student says too much, gently interrupt: "Stop, stop. Don't tell it how to solve the problem. Just describe the problem."

Step 3: Ask ChatGPT for a Lovable Prompt

Once ChatGPT understands your idea, ask:

"I want to use Lovable to build a fully functioning app. Let's start with just a simple page with good copy, right colors, right design. Give me the Lovable prompt."

Note: Lovable has around 5000 character limit for prompts. When you say "give me a Lovable prompt," ChatGPT knows to keep it within that limit.

Step 4: Paste into Lovable

Copy everything ChatGPT gave you and paste directly into Lovable. Don't change a thing.

  • Let Lovable generate the app (narrate while waiting)
  • Show the preview - students see a working app!
  • Use "Visual Edits" mode to make quick changes by clicking

Step 5: Go Back to ChatGPT for the Next Step

Once your first version is done, return to ChatGPT:

"Great, first step done. Now give me the second step."

Repeat this cycle until your app is complete. It's a dance between ChatGPT (vision) and Lovable (execution).

The Compound Effect: "Every little win compounds. You may start by building a presentation. Then you realize you can build an entire website. Then you realize you can send transcripts into vibe coding software and build notes. The time from idea to creation shrinks. Your ideas become working prototypes in less than one day. Sometimes in half an hour."

Pre-Class Preparation

24 Hours Before

  • Test your Lovable account - ensure you have credits available
  • Create a fresh project for the demo (so you start clean)
  • Test Supabase connection in a sample project
  • Have backup screenshots ready in case of technical issues
  • Review the slide deck and practice transitions

1 Hour Before

  • Open Lovable in browser, logged in and ready
  • Open Supabase dashboard in a separate tab
  • Close unnecessary browser tabs and applications
  • Test screen sharing with slide deck
  • Have the curriculum document open for reference
  • Prepare a glass of water

Technical Setup

  • Browser: Chrome or Edge recommended (best compatibility with Lovable)
  • Screen resolution: 1920x1080 or higher for clear demos
  • Zoom/screen share: Use "Optimize for video" if available
  • Tabs to have open:
    • Slide deck
    • ChatGPT (for brainstorming)
    • Lovable (fresh project)
    • Supabase dashboard
    • Unsplash.com (for free images)
    • The live example apps (for inspiration)

Recommended Tools to Mention

  • Whisper Flow (wispr.flow.ai): Voice-to-text for any app. "You're speaking, not typing." Works in WhatsApp, emails, everywhere. For Android, recommend "Dictate."
  • Unsplash: Free library of professional images. Never use AI-generated images when you can use real ones.
  • Replet: More advanced than Lovable, good for apps that need OpenAI integration. Has "Add OpenAI to your app" as a managed service.
Important: Always have a backup plan. If Lovable or Supabase has issues during the live demo, be prepared to show screenshots or a pre-recorded video of the key steps.

Class 1: Foundations of Vibe Coding

Slides 1-3 Opening & Hook

Timing: 5 minutes

  • Start with energy! This is the "wow" moment.
  • Show the live example apps immediately - don't make students wait.
  • Key message: "These were built by people just like you, with zero coding experience."
  • Let students click around the examples for 30 seconds if time permits.
Instructor tip: Ask students: "What would YOU build if you could create any app?" This plants the seed early.

Slides 4-7 What is Vibe Coding?

Timing: 8 minutes

  • Define vibe coding as "describing what you want in plain language."
  • Emphasize: This is NOT traditional coding. You're having a conversation with AI.
  • Use the Andrej Karpathy quote - it adds credibility.
  • When showing the infrastructure diagram, keep it simple: "These are just layers that work together. You don't need to understand the technical details."
Avoid: Don't get too technical with the infrastructure explanation. Students just need to know the layers exist, not how they work internally.

Slides 8-10 Why Lovable?

Timing: 5 minutes

  • Position Lovable as the "all-in-one solution" - this is its key advantage.
  • Briefly mention alternatives (Bolt, Cursor) but don't dwell on them.
  • Key selling points: Supabase built-in, GitHub sync, one-click deploy.
  • If students ask "Why not X?" - say Lovable is optimized for non-developers and has the best UX.

Slides 11-15 The VIBE Framework

Timing: 10 minutes

  • This is the most important teaching moment in Class 1.
  • Go through each letter slowly: Vision, Interaction, Backend, Experience.
  • Use the "Stress Type Quiz" example to illustrate each component.
  • Have students mentally apply VIBE to their own idea (give them 60 seconds to think).
Engagement technique: Ask students to type one word describing their app idea in the chat. This keeps them engaged and thinking about applications.

Slides 16-25 Live Demo: Stress Type Quiz

Timing: 25 minutes

  • This is the main event. Take your time and narrate every step.
  • Step 1: Show the VIBE prompt being pasted into Lovable.
  • Step 2: Let Lovable generate the app (narrate what's happening).
  • Step 3: Show the preview - students should see a working app!
  • Step 4: Make an iteration - change a color or add a feature with natural language.
  • Step 5: Connect Supabase - show how easy the integration is.
  • Step 6: Deploy and share the live URL.
If something breaks: Don't panic! This is a learning moment. Say: "This is why iteration is so important in vibe coding. Let me show you how we fix this..." Then use natural language to debug.

Slides 26-28 Wrap-up & Preview

Timing: 7 minutes

  • Recap the VIBE framework - have students say each letter with you.
  • Share resources: Lovable docs, example prompts, community links.
  • Tease Class 2: "Next time, we add user accounts and persistent data!"
  • Encourage students to experiment before next class.

Q&A Session (30 minutes)

  • Group common questions into themes before answering.
  • If you don't know an answer, say: "Great question - let me find out and follow up."
  • Encourage students to share their screen if they've tried something.

Class 2: Dynamic Apps & Databases

Slides 29-31 Welcome Back & Recap

Timing: 5 minutes

  • Quick energy check - ask students to share one thing they tried since Class 1.
  • Speed recap of VIBE framework (should take 90 seconds max).
  • Set the stage: "Today we're adding real power - user accounts and databases."

Slides 32-36 Understanding Databases

Timing: 10 minutes

  • Use the spreadsheet analogy - everyone understands spreadsheets.
  • Tables = Sheets, Fields = Columns, Records = Rows.
  • Show a real Supabase table view - it looks like a spreadsheet!
  • Explain Row Level Security in simple terms: "Each user only sees their own data."
Teaching tip: Draw the connection to real apps they use: "Instagram stores your posts in a table. Spotify stores your playlists. You're about to do the same thing."

Slides 37-40 User Authentication

Timing: 8 minutes

  • Keep it simple: "Sign up, log in, stay logged in across devices."
  • Supabase handles all the security - students don't need to worry about passwords.
  • Show the Supabase Auth dashboard - users appear here automatically.
  • Mention email verification and magic links as options.

Slides 41-50 Live Demo: Habit Tracker

Timing: 30 minutes

  • This demo is more complex - narrate even more carefully.
  • Step 1: Start with the VIBE prompt for the Habit Tracker.
  • Step 2: Show initial generation (no database yet).
  • Step 3: Connect Supabase and enable auth.
  • Step 4: Create the habits table (show both Lovable and Supabase views).
  • Step 5: Test sign-up with a real email.
  • Step 6: Add a habit, show it persisting.
  • Step 7: Log out and log back in - data is still there!
  • Step 8: Deploy to production.
Common issues: Row Level Security can be tricky. If data isn't showing, check that RLS policies are set up correctly in Supabase. Have a pre-configured project as backup.

Slides 51-55 API Keys & External Services

Timing: 10 minutes

  • Explain APIs as "doors to other services."
  • Show where to get an OpenAI API key (don't show your actual key!).
  • Demonstrate adding the key to Lovable's environment variables.
  • Quick example: Add an AI-powered feature to the Habit Tracker.
Security reminder: Never share API keys on screen or in recordings. Use placeholder text like "sk-xxx" when showing where keys go.

Slides 56-60 Next Steps & Closing

Timing: 7 minutes

  • Celebrate what students have learned - they can now build real apps!
  • Share the idea generation framework for finding their first project.
  • Provide community resources and support channels.
  • End with inspiration: "Your first app doesn't have to be perfect. It just has to exist."

Q&A Session (30 minutes)

  • Expect more technical questions in this session.
  • Common topics: pricing, limits, specific integrations.
  • Encourage students to share ideas - facilitate peer feedback.

Common Questions & Answers

Q: How much does Lovable cost?
Lovable has a free tier that's great for learning and prototyping. Paid plans start around $20/month and give you more projects and features. For most people starting out, the free tier is enough to build your first few apps.
Q: Can I build a mobile app with Lovable?
Lovable builds web apps that work great on mobile browsers. They're "responsive" which means they adapt to any screen size. For a true native mobile app (in the App Store), you'd need additional tools, but for most use cases, a mobile-friendly web app works perfectly.
Q: What if I want to hire a developer later?
Great question! Lovable syncs your code to GitHub, which is the industry standard. Any developer can take that code and continue building on it. You're not locked into Lovable forever.
Q: How do I handle payments in my app?
You can integrate Stripe by getting an API key and connecting it through Lovable. It's one of the most common integrations. I'd recommend building your core app first, then adding payments as a second step.
Q: Is my data secure?
Supabase uses enterprise-grade security, including encryption and Row Level Security. It's the same technology used by major companies. That said, always follow best practices - don't store sensitive data you don't need, and use strong authentication.
Q: What happens if Lovable changes or shuts down?
Your code is yours. It's stored in GitHub and follows standard coding practices. You could take that code and host it anywhere, or continue development with a traditional developer. You're not trapped.
Q: How is this different from Wix or Squarespace?
Those are great for static websites and landing pages. Vibe coding with Lovable lets you build actual applications - things with user accounts, databases, business logic, and integrations. It's the difference between a brochure and a product.
Q: Do I need to know any coding at all?
No! That's the whole point of vibe coding. You describe what you want in plain English, and the AI handles the technical implementation. That said, over time you might pick up some terminology - and that's a bonus, not a requirement.

Language Guidelines

The words we use shape how students perceive this technology. Here's how to frame concepts:

✓ Say This
  • "Vibe coding"
  • "Describe what you want"
  • "Have a conversation with AI"
  • "Iterate and refine"
  • "Your AI co-builder"
  • "Build real applications"
  • "Anyone can do this"
✗ Avoid This
  • "No-code" (implies limitation)
  • "Write code"
  • "Programming"
  • "Debug" (say "fix" or "improve")
  • "Technical skills required"
  • "It's complicated"
  • "You need to learn..."

Tone & Energy

  • Enthusiastic but grounded. Be excited about the technology without overselling.
  • Empowering, not intimidating. Students should feel capable, not overwhelmed.
  • Honest about limitations. It's okay to say "Vibe coding is great for X, but not ideal for Y."
  • Celebratory of progress. Acknowledge every small win students make.

Troubleshooting Guide

During Live Demos

Issue: Lovable is generating slowly
Keep talking! Narrate what's happening: "The AI is thinking through the architecture..." Use this time to explain what's being built. If it takes more than 60 seconds, acknowledge it: "Sometimes the AI needs a moment - let's look at what it's building..." You can also switch to another volunteer's demo while waiting.
Issue: AI-generated images look fake
Show students how to replace with real images from Unsplash:
  1. Go to unsplash.com and search for what you need
  2. Click "Share" and copy the link
  3. Tell Lovable: "Replace the image behind [text] with this new image" and paste the link
Key message: "Never use AI-generated images when you can use real ones. Unsplash is free."
Issue: ChatGPT is generating too much junk
"ChatGPT sometimes gets super excited, and then it wants to show off, so it goes and designs these BS screens." Just ignore it! Say: "Don't worry about all this junk. Just ask it to give you the first instructional prompt." Stay focused on the immediate next step.
Issue: The generated app looks different than expected
This is actually a teaching moment! Say: "This is why iteration is key in vibe coding. Let me show you how we refine this..." Then demonstrate making changes with natural language prompts.
Issue: Supabase connection fails
Check that you've clicked "Enable Supabase" in Lovable settings. Verify the project URL and anon key are correct. If it still fails, show screenshots of a working connection and move on.
Issue: Authentication isn't working
Ensure email confirmations are disabled in Supabase Auth settings (for demo purposes). Check that auth is enabled in the Lovable project. Test with a fresh email address.
Issue: Data isn't saving or showing
Row Level Security might be blocking access. Check that RLS policies are set up in Supabase. For demos, you can temporarily disable RLS (but explain this is just for testing).

Student-Side Issues

Issue: Student can't sign up for Lovable
Have them try a different email or browser. Incognito mode often helps. Worst case, they can watch and follow along, then try later.
Issue: Student's app won't deploy
Check for error messages in Lovable. Often it's a simple fix like a missing dependency. Have them share their screen so you can diagnose together.
Issue: Student is frustrated / overwhelmed
Validate their feelings: "This is new, and it's okay to feel challenged." Remind them that iteration is part of the process. Offer to help during Q&A or office hours.

Important Caveats to Share

Be honest with students about what vibe coding can and cannot do:

Prototypes vs. Production Apps
"What we're doing is building a prototype. To build a functioning app for the public, that can be more complex. You gotta worry about security, hosting, all of these different things. If you don't do security right, you could get hacked. Someone could steal your customer's emails."
When to Still Hire a Developer
"Launching an app to the public requires testing, compliance, deployment on the App Store. You may still hire a developer for that - but your cost is gonna go down by more than 50% because you've already built the prototype."
Scope of These Classes
"Building a clickable prototype that you can take to a developer who can build a functioning demo - that's what we're doing. It's way beyond the scope of this class to build production-ready public apps. But you CAN easily build webpages - like the supplement website I showed you. There's nothing to hack on a landing page."
