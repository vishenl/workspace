Key insight: The top 4 hooks share a common structure — they open with a bold, specific claim or story, demonstrate AI doing something visually tangible, and end with an emotional punchline that connects to the viewer's life (career, health, daily tasks). None of them teach a framework or technique.

5 Patterns That Work

1

Fear of being left behind beats everything

The "College degrees are useless" hook generated $384K — more than the bottom 10 hooks combined ($277K). The emotional trigger is existential: career survival. The audience buys to avoid falling behind, not just to learn a cool tool.

Evidence: #1 in revenue, #4 in ROAS (85%). Dubai hook also works on fear ("I stopped hiring").

2

Vishen-narrated personal stories outperform instruction

Every hook where Vishen tells a personal story (doctor, Dubai, family breakfast, Adam historian) is in the top half. Every hook featuring a third-party instructor or technique explanation is in the bottom half. Authority and personal connection matter enormously.

Evidence: Top 8 hooks are all Vishen-led stories/demos. Bottom 5 are technique-focused.

3

Specific, visual AI demos with real-time results convert

Top demos share a pattern: photograph something real (fridge, food, eye infection, shopping list) → show ChatGPT analyzing it → reveal a surprising/useful result. The audience sees the AI working on something tangible.

Evidence: Nutrition Coach ($267K), Omelette v1 (86% ROAS), Doctor (82% ROAS) all use this formula.

4

Universal daily-life use cases > niche creative use cases

Health, food, shopping, career = universal. Songwriting (54%), blog writing (36-48%) = niche. The Amplify audience is everyday people who want AI to make life easier, not writers and musicians seeking creative tools.

Evidence: 59-point ROAS gap between best universal hook (95%) and worst niche hook (36%).

5

Entertainment value drives efficiency

The "Adam" historian hook has the 2nd-highest ROAS (89%) because it's genuinely fun — it reads like a story, not a tutorial. The ACE Framework (64%) and One-Two-Three Technique (36%) are pure education with zero entertainment, and they're near the bottom.

Evidence: Adam (89% ROAS) vs ACE Framework (64%) — same format (educational), wildly different engagement.

4 Patterns That Don't Work

1

Teaching frameworks and techniques

ACE Framework (64%), One-Two-Three Technique (36%), Blog Collaboration (48%) are the three worst ROAS performers. Audiences don't want to learn a methodology in a hook — they want to see a result. These feel like sitting in a classroom.

2

Duplicate variants of the same hook

Running two versions of three hooks is diluting spend. Omelette v1 (86%) vs v2 (64%) = 22-point gap. Blog Writing v1 (36%) vs v2 (48%) = both bad. Goal Advisor v1 (73%) vs v2 (70%) = both mediocre. The variants aren't finding new audiences.

3

Product-pitch style hooks

"Hands-On AI Skills Training" (77% ROAS, only $11K rev) is the most direct product pitch. Low revenue suggests the audience isn't ready to hear about the product before seeing value. The best hooks never mention Amplify until the very end.

4

Niche creative use cases

Songwriting with Suno (54% ROAS, $8K rev) appeals to a narrow slice of the audience. Blog writing hooks (36-48%) are similarly niche. The audience that converts is looking for life-applicable AI skills, not creative-tool tutorials.

Recommendations

Scale Now

  • "20-Hour Workweek" (95% ROAS, $52K rev) — Highest ROAS in the dataset but under-spent. This is likely the biggest immediate opportunity. Increase budget significantly.
  • "AI Replaced My Doctor" (82% ROAS, $32K rev) — Health/empathy angle is compelling and under-exploited. The Nutrition Coach ($267K) proves the health vertical converts.
  • "Omelette Customizer v1" (86% ROAS, $42K rev) — Strong efficiency, family-friendly, relatable. Cut v2 and reallocate its budget here.

Do More Of

  • Fear/urgency hooks. Test: "AI is replacing [specific jobs]", "What your boss isn't telling you about AI", "The 3 industries AI will destroy first."
  • Vishen personal stories with AI. Mine real life: travel, parenting, business decisions, health — any situation where ChatGPT solved a real problem unexpectedly.
  • "Photograph something → AI analyzes it" formula. Test: bookshelf, closet, calendar, medicine cabinet, grocery receipt.

Do Less Of / Kill

  • Kill both blog writing hooks. 36% and 48% ROAS. Worst performers. Reallocate immediately.
  • Kill the songwriting hook. 54% ROAS, $8K rev. Too niche.
  • Kill the ACE Framework hook. 64% ROAS, $12K rev. The framework-teaching approach doesn't convert.
  • Stop running duplicate variants of underperforming hooks. Pick winners, cut the rest.

Test Next

  • "I stopped doing X because of AI" — The Dubai "I stopped hiring" punchline drove massive revenue. Build more hooks around this frame.
  • "AI vs. my [professional]" — Doctor hook worked. Test: accountant, trainer, therapist, tutor, travel agent.
  • Stat-driven fear hooks — "256% improvement in 2 years" landed in the doctor hook. Lead with shocking AI growth stats.
  • Family/kids + AI — The omelette hook with Hayden and Eve scored 86% ROAS. The family angle adds warmth to the tech topic.

Winning Hook Formula

Reverse-engineered from the top 6 performers.

Step 1: Bold opener. Start with a provocative claim or personal anecdote. "College degrees are officially useless." "I replaced my doctor with AI." "I stopped all hiring."

Step 2: Visual, tangible demo. Show AI analyzing something real — a photo of your food, your fridge, your eye infection, your shopping list. The audience needs to SEE the AI working, not hear about it abstractly.

Step 3: Surprising result + emotional punchline. "It said buy the backpack first so you can carry everything else." "It had more empathy than my doctor." "It remembered Hayden likes thin omelettes." The punchline makes AI feel smart, personal, and indispensable.

Step 4: Stakes escalation. Zoom out from the personal story to the macro: "This is how fast AI is changing." "Are you ready for this?" "Your life is going to be amazing — or disrupted." The hook ends with personal relevance to the viewer.
