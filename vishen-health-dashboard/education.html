🔬 Key Concepts Explained

Why SHBG Matters

Sex Hormone Binding Globulin (SHBG) binds to testosterone, rendering it biologically inactive. Your SHBG of 66.9 nmol/L is HIGH, meaning much of your testosterone is "locked up" and unavailable.

The Problem: Even with normal total testosterone, high SHBG = low bioavailable testosterone. This affects energy, muscle mass, libido, and cognitive function.

The Solution: Boron reduces SHBG by 25% by inhibiting SHBG gene expression. Stinging Nettle Root blocks SHBG binding sites. Together: 30-40% increase in free testosterone.

Cholesterol Management

Your LDL: 171 mg/dL (Target: <110). LDL particles oxidize and deposit in arteries, causing atherosclerosis. This is the #1 cause of heart attacks and strokes.

Natural Approach: Red Yeast Rice contains monacolin K (natural lovastatin). Clinical studies show 20-30% LDL reduction. Bergamot (citrus extract) inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, raises HDL, and reduces oxidized LDL.

Critical: Red Yeast Rice depletes CoQ10 (just like pharmaceutical statins). Always take 200mg CoQ10 to prevent muscle pain and mitochondrial dysfunction.

Vision Protection & Macular Degeneration

Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD) is the leading cause of blindness after age 50. Currently, you have NO targeted vision supplementation (score: 45/100).

The Science: Lutein and Zeaxanthin are carotenoids that accumulate in the macula. They filter blue light (like internal sunglasses) and protect retinal cells from oxidative damage. Clinical trials (AREDS2) show 50% reduction in AMD progression.

The Stack: Lutein (20mg) + Zeaxanthin (4mg) + Astaxanthin (12mg) + Bilberry (160mg) = comprehensive macular protection, reduced eye fatigue, improved night vision.

Testosterone Optimization (Non-TRT)

Your Free T: 23.9 pg/mL (Optimal: 35-50). Free testosterone is what matters for muscle, energy, libido, and cognition.

Three-Pronged Approach:
1. Reduce SHBG (Boron + Stinging Nettle) → frees existing T
2. Increase production (Tongkat Ali) → more total T
3. Support pathways (Zinc, Vitamin D, Magnesium) → optimize synthesis

Expected Results: 15-25% free T increase within 3 months. Natural, sustainable, without shutting down endogenous production (unlike TRT).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Why 40 supplements instead of just a multivitamin?

Multivitamins contain tiny doses of many nutrients - not therapeutic amounts. They're "insurance" for deficiencies, not optimization tools. Targeted supplementation uses clinical doses proven effective in research. Example: Multivitamins have 10-20mg lutein; clinical studies showing AMD protection use 20mg+ daily.

How long until I see results?

Week 1-2: Improved energy, better sleep (Magnesium, Ashwagandha)
Month 1: Reduced eye fatigue (vision stack), initial testosterone changes
Month 3: Measurable blood work improvements (SHBG, LDL, Free T)
Month 6: All biomarkers optimized, sustained benefits

Can I take all supplements together?

No. Timing matters for absorption and synergy. Fat-soluble vitamins (D, A, E, K) need dietary fat. Some minerals compete for absorption (zinc/calcium). See the Daily Protocol page for optimized timing. General rule: spread throughout day with meals.

Are these supplements safe long-term?

Yes, at recommended doses. All supplements in this protocol have extensive safety data. Red Yeast Rice requires periodic liver enzyme monitoring (just like pharmaceutical statins). Retest blood work every 3-6 months to monitor efficacy and adjust dosages. Always inform your physician about all supplements.

What if I miss a day?

Not a problem. These are daily nutrients building up in tissues over time, not pharmaceuticals requiring strict adherence. Just resume normal schedule next day. Don't double up doses. Consistency over weeks/months matters more than perfection every day.

📖 Glossary of Terms

SHBG (Sex Hormone Binding Globulin)

Protein produced by liver that binds to sex hormones (testosterone, estrogen). High SHBG = less bioavailable testosterone.

Free Testosterone

Testosterone not bound to SHBG or albumin. Only 2-3% of total testosterone. This is the biologically active form.

LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein)

"Bad cholesterol." Transports cholesterol to arteries where it can oxidize and cause plaque. Target: <100 mg/dL.

HDL (High-Density Lipoprotein)

"Good cholesterol." Removes excess cholesterol from arteries back to liver. Higher is better. Target: >60 mg/dL.

HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)

3-month average of blood glucose levels. <5.7% = excellent glucose control. >6.5% = diabetes.

CRP (C-Reactive Protein)

Marker of systemic inflammation. <1 mg/L = low risk. >3 mg/L = high cardiovascular risk.

Adaptogen

Natural substance that helps body adapt to stress. Examples: Ashwagandha, Rhodiola. Non-specific, regulates homeostasis.
