Foundation

The 6 Core Principles

Master these before writing a single word

6
Principle 1

Keep it Tight but Leave Them Hungry

The Problem

People often feel masterclass content is enough to practice without purchasing the course.

The Solution

Give real value, but create strategic incompleteness. They should think "I need more of this" not "I've got enough."

Principle 2

Let Them Experience a Breakthrough Immediately

Key Insight

"Result in Advance" isn't advance enough if it takes days or weeks to see results. Design exercises that create direct, immediate results—something they feel RIGHT NOW in the masterclass.

Ask yourself: What shift or result can they experience in the next 10 minutes?

Principle 3

Explain the Cost of NOT Buying

"How much does it cost—how much is it worth—to let your life stay the same?"

— Christie Marie Sheldon (Love or Above)

Be honest, sharp, and direct—but not salesy. Make inaction feel expensive.

Principles 4, 5, and 6

4

Objection-Case Study Pattern

Pair objections with case studies of people who had the SAME objection and overcame it. Proof that the objection doesn't have to stop them.

5

Sprinkle Social Proof Throughout

Don't save all testimonials for one section. Scatter short success stories throughout the entire masterclass. Constant validation builds cumulative belief.

6

Story-Content Looping

From Lisa Nichols: Tell engaging stories that grip the audience, then lead to a lesson. Use "The Valley" (the dip before the rise) to create emotional investment.

Part 1

The Psychology of the Opening

Why This Matters

The first 7 minutes determine whether someone stays or leaves. You're competing against Netflix, social media, and a million distractions. Your job is to create an emotional hook that makes them feel "I'm in the right place" while building enough credibility and curiosity to keep them watching.

Energy is contagious—if you're excited, they'll feel it.

The Balance: Excitement + Authenticity

Excitement Without Authenticity

= Salesy, fake, off-putting

Authenticity Without Excitement

= Boring, low energy, no hook

You Need BOTH

Share the actual honest story of WHY you chose this author. Be vulnerable about your struggles. But also bring the excitement of what they're about to experience.

The Opening Pattern

"I don't know how you got here... but I know you're here to [TITLE] and [SUBTITLE]"

What you're doing is getting them back in the state that got them to register.

People register days or weeks in advance. By the time they show up, they may have forgotten WHY they were excited. This re-activates that original desire.

The Credibility Stack

Personal Results

Visual proof that backs up your claims—statistics, awards, results, recognition

Positioning

Books, associations, companies, experience linked to something they know and respect

Celebrity

Famous people with visual proof? Niche heroes you can link yourself to?

Testimonials

They may believe YOU can do it, but can THEY? Show people like them who succeeded

Social Proof: The P.A.R. Formula

Hit them with 5 case studies in the first 90 seconds

P - Problem

What was their situation before?

A - Application

What did they do/learn?

R - Result

What happened after?

"How did this pharmacist from Adelaide change her life after just 3 days? And how did this single mom from Ohio..."

Get Them to Commit

The Commitment & Consistency Principle (Cialdini)

Once people make a small commitment, they're more likely to follow through on larger ones. Getting them to say "yes" to paying attention makes them more likely to say "yes" to buying later.

  1. Ask them to commit to paying attention ("Showing up vs Playing Full Out")
  2. Tell them to eliminate distractions—close Skype, FB, Instagram
  3. Get them saying "Yes" in their head. Make it fun: "Hell yeah!", "Count me in!", "Woo Hoo!"

Part 2

The Valley Model (Lisa Nichols)

"Before I became who I am, you should know that..."

Why This Works

Before people will buy a solution, they need to feel the WEIGHT of the problem. The author's vulnerability creates trust and relatability. Their "valley" moment shows they understand the struggle.

Always include: The author's BIG WHY—their mission and why they do what they do.

The 8 Categories of Objections

Choose 3-5 that apply to your product

Belief

Why should this time be different?

Ability

Could I even learn this?

Trust

Too good to be true?

Fear

Fear of change, failure, unknown

Conflict

Betraying who I am?

Confusion

Where do I even start?

Timing

Is now the right time?

Length

How long will this take?

The 3-Step Objection Reframe

  1. Acknowledge & Soften: "You may be wondering if you can or can't do what I'm going to share with you today."
  2. Validate & Reframe: "That is a valid concern because what you really want to know is... will this time be different?"
  3. Present a Target: "I believe you can, and as we go through, I'm going to show you how this works for people from all backgrounds with their own unique challenges."

Give Hope: Speak to the Little Voice

Key Technique

You never speak to them directly. You speak to the little voice in them. "Now you may be saying to yourself..." Nothing is as powerful as their self-talk.

"Imagine if you mastered this skill, where could you be 3 years from now? 3 months?"

Part 3

Present the BRULE (Bullshit Rule)

Pattern Interrupt

Before people can accept a new solution, they need to reject the old one. Identify a common belief that's been holding them back. When the old paradigm crumbles, they're ready to embrace something new.

What is the Common Belief?

What does everyone think is the solution that isn't actually working?

What is the Common Enemy?

What external force keeps this false belief alive?

Part 4

Choose the Right Emotional States

The emotional state someone is in determines whether they'll buy. Ask: "What sections will keep people in the BEST emotional states to buy later?"

States That Kill Conversion

  • Fear: If scared, they won't buy
  • Confusion: Confused minds don't buy

States That Drive Conversion

  • Enthusiasm: Act on this TONIGHT
  • Safety: Trust creates safety
  • Anger (Channeled): At the common enemy
  • Connection: You're not alone

Go DEEP on Step 1

Teach the most important item first (even if it's out of order)

  1. Explain the concept
  2. Give an exercise
  3. Share a case study of client success
  4. Share another case study
  5. Recap the step

Then gloss over Steps 2-5 with case studies and trial closes.

Part 5

The Two Paths

Reduce Complexity to a Binary Choice

The biggest mistake they can make is ignoring the information and NOT applying it. That's the real danger. By framing two clear paths, you make the decision simple.

Path 1: Do Nothing

Everything stays the same. Continue the struggle.

Path 2: Take Action

Live in alignment with this knowledge. Experience true [BENEFITS].

The Yes Momentum

Ask 6 questions to get 6 yesses

Commitment & Consistency

When you can't see their face, use this phrase: "I want to ask you a couple of questions and all I want to know is: Does the word 'YES' pop into your mind?"

  • "Do you feel confident harnessing the power of X?"
  • "Were you happy you came for the webinar today?"
  • "Can you see yourself coming back to these lessons?"
  • "Would you say this time was well spent?"
  • "The power of this technique is in its simplicity. Agree?"

The penalty for waiting

The Price Drop Ladder

Don't go from $4,000 to $399. Step down gradually.

  1. Anchor with high comparison ($8,000 live event)
  2. First drop (Regular price: $1,500)
  3. Second drop (Special price: $995)
  4. Webinar Unlock Price (Today only: $495)

"Never say SAVE. Say 'You should be LUCKY to get this at this price. I negotiated a special deal.'"

— Jason Fladlien

The Call to Action is a COMMAND

Why People Don't Buy

Most people who don't buy at this point don't buy because they don't know what you want them to do next. Be VERY specific.

The Tone: Like a good friend helping someone make an important decision—gentle but firm.

"Listen, I'm not going to let you go back and regret this later. You're going to come with me..."

Part 7

Crush Objections

Pattern interrupts, scarcity, and final thoughts

7
Part 7

The Pattern Interrupt Formula

  1. Approach the objection head-on BEFORE they bring it up: "You might be thinking this is too expensive..."
  2. Redirect with something UNEXPECTED: "...but not more expensive than [X]"
  3. Tie it back to your solution

Golden Rule

NEVER argue or pressure your prospect. This causes them to reject you out of their reality bubble. Instead, respect their reality, enter it, and do a subtle shift.

Engineer Real Scarcity

The Rule

Don't FAKE scarcity—ENGINEER it into your business model. Offers without scarcity don't sell as well. The fear of loss is always greater than the desire to succeed.

What They've Lost

"Every day you're not sleeping, you're cutting performance by 40%"

Take Away Bonuses

"Past tomorrow at 2pm, you will no longer get the bonuses"

Take Away Opportunity

Create genuine urgency with deadlines

Key Takeaways

  • Psychology drives everything: Understand WHY each section works

  • Go deep on one thing: Then gloss over the rest

  • CTA is a command: Be specific, be a friend, be firm
