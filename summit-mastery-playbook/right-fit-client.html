THREE LENSES

Demographics vs Psychographics vs Valuegraphics

Demographics (what we have)

Age, gender, income, location, job title.

"This is the starting point, not the answer."

Psychographics (what we need)

What they believe, fear, and desire. Their self-image. How they see the world.

"Being AI-curious is not a reason to buy."

Valuegraphics (the secret weapon)

What do they VALUE most? Time with family. Personal growth. Financial freedom. Simplicity.

"Your audience has teenage kids. Weekends are family time. They won't come Saturday. A religious demographic won't come Sunday morning."

"When I think of right-fit clients, a lot of people tell me demographics. What I also want to know are the psychographics and the valuegraphics."

CASE STUDY

The Garrett White Example — Extreme Right-Fit Client

Garrett's Right-Fit Client Profile

  • A man in his 40s-50s who met his wife in high school
  • He's been going to church since he was a kid, coaches little league, attends daughter's recitals
  • He's a "rule follower" — loves following rules
  • Small business owner or upper management
  • He noticed his wife is more interested in Pinterest than him at dinner
  • His kids are glued to TikTok
  • His pastor doesn't have answers for the "new rules"
  • Bedroom has gone cold — hasn't been intimate in over a year
  • Started eating more, drinking more, gained weight
  • His deepest, darkest secret: he turned to porn and felt awful about it
  • All he wants is: "Where are the rules? There's a new rule book and I don't know who has it."

How Garrett Used This

Made men buy specific shirts. Enforced rules at the event — "Wrong shirt, go change or you're kicked out." His RIGHT-fit clients LOVED it: "Rules! Thank God!" His WRONG-fit clients thought he was insane.

The celebrities at Garrett's event weren't famous people — they were regular guys who looked like the audience but had transformed.

The lesson: When you know your person THAT well, you can design every touchpoint to serve them.

"The more you know the person, the more you can structure the marketing at every stage. Shoot at everything, hit nothing."

NEXT STEPS

Survey Refinement Recommendations

Actionable improvements to sharpen our understanding of the right-fit client.

✅
Separate "CEO" from "Solopreneur" from "Founder"
They're completely different buyer profiles. Bari has a classification system to share that distinguishes these segments properly.
✅
Add "WHY" Questions
"Why are you curious about AI?" Not just "Are you curious?" Being curious is not a reason to buy.
✅
Ask About Team Size
Solo is vastly different from 2 people, 5 people, 10 people. This changes the product fit and messaging entirely.
✅
Survey the Full 2.5M Email List
Don't just survey existing buyers — survey the full list. Who do you WANT to attract, not just who already bought?
✅
Mine Existing Data
Export event chat, run through AI for sentiment + trending topics, but ALSO read it manually yourself. Both perspectives matter.
✅
Analyze by Speaker
What were comments on each specific speaker? Which speakers drove engagement with the right-fit client vs. the wrong-fit client?
✅
Look at Fulfillment Calls and Customer Care
What questions do they ask? Where do they get stuck? What excites them? This is a goldmine of psychographic data.

"AI can aggregate it for you and give you fast information, and then there's the human component of reading the tea leaves. I want you to do both."

HOUR 4 INSIGHT

The Two Banks of Solopreneurs

Target: Busy & Successful
  • Has a real business generating revenue
  • Desperately short on time
  • Can afford to invest in solutions
  • Knows the pain of scaling with humans
  • This is Julie.
Not Target: Aspiring & Time-Rich
  • No business yet, or very early stage
  • Lots of time, limited funds
  • Looking for how to START, not scale
  • Needs a different product entirely
  • Not our right-fit client.

The Zero-Overhead Pitch

Wrong message: "You'll either use AI to grow or be replaced by those who do." — This is employee fear, not solopreneur fear.

Right message: "You literally have zero overhead. All you need is a computer and this knowledge. You never have to hire another employee. Every dollar you make, you keep. No healthcare, no payroll, no 401k, no complaining, no onboarding, no offboarding, no they-took-your-secrets-and-started-competing."

"There are 2 banks of solopreneurs: I have no business so I have tons of time, or I have lots of business so I have no time. The one we want is the person with business and no time — because they can afford it, and they know the pain."

HOUR 5 Q&A INSIGHT

How to Research Actual Buyers

Better Survey Questions
  • Not "Do you use AI?" but "How many hours have you spent on ChatGPT?"
  • "ChatGPT or Claude — which do you prefer?"
  • Short forms, super simple
  • "Is the question you're asking gonna give you the answer you want?"
The Balance Equation

50% about you, 50% about them. Who you want to serve TRUMPS what they want from you. If Julie only wants basic ChatGPT and you'd be bored — that's not right fit. It has to be a mix of both.

"Surveys tell you what they think they need. Your job is to figure out the actual solution. What they'll tell you and what you know they need are often two different things — sell them what they want, give them what they need."
