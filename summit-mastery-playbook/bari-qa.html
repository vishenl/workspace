Live Sales Lobby — Tech Setup & Execution

Bari's team uses the Obio platform for live sales lobbies. The key interface element: a prominent "Talk to Program Expert" button visible during and after the pitch window.

The lobby infrastructure requires:

  • Host + 2 talks + 2 chats — the minimum lobby support team
  • Breakout rooms as mini-lobbies — smaller rooms managed by a switchboard operator who routes attendees
  • FAQ bot with human vetting — a bot trained on common questions, but every response is reviewed by a human before it reaches the prospect
  • Customer-to-salesperson ratio varies — anywhere from 20:1 to 100:1 depending on the sales team's ability level

The breakout room strategy is critical for creating intimacy at scale. Attendees don't see the total headcount — they only see their small room.

"By putting them into smaller rooms, they can't tell how many people are in there, and it feels more intimate."

How to Research Actual Buyers (Not Just Registrants)

Start by streamlining existing stats — you likely already have data you're not using. Then deploy a new simplified survey, but craft questions with extreme care.

  • Specific questions: "How many hours do you spend on ChatGPT per week?" not "Do you use AI?"
  • "Reading the tea leaves" conversations — talk to concierges, hosts, and coaches who interact with buyers daily for psychographic intel

The survey should be half about you, half about them. But there's a critical principle:

Key Principle: "Who you want to serve trumps what they want from you." Your vision for who you serve matters more than pure demand data.

"If Julie would say I only want to learn ChatGPT and never go beyond, and you'd be bored — then that's not right fit. It has to be a mix of what they want and what you want."

Celebrity Speaker Integration

Bari's stance is unambiguous: "Completely unnecessary. Can sometimes backfire. I personally don't like it."

Instead, build celebrities INSIDE your community. Elevate your own people into the spotlight.

The Exception: A celebrity keynote on Morning Day 2 can work for crossover audiences. Joe Dispenza promoted to his own list, brought a crossover audience, and drove registrations. But he doesn't need to be IN the mastery program.

The critical distinction:

  • Marie Forleo = Good — she teaches IN the mastery program. Her summit presence aligns with what buyers get.
  • Joe Dispenza = Risky — he doesn't teach in the program. His presence at the summit makes the mastery look incomplete without him.
The Rule: Not every speaker has to be in mastery, but it's a mistake when NONE of them are.

The Perfect 3-Day Flow (PHD Model)

This was Bari's most detailed answer — the complete architecture for a high-converting 3-day event.

The Event Architecture

Opening Warm-up

Sets the emotional tone. Establishes the container. Gets the audience into the right state before any content begins.

Wrap-up (Ties with a Bow)

The final session connects every thread back together. No loose ends. The audience leaves with one unified takeaway.

The Trifecta (Most Important Sequence)

Panel & Invitation
→
Laser Coaching
→
Repitch
  • Panel & Invitation: "Most people think the offer is most important. It's really just getting the offer onto the table." The panel creates social proof, then the invitation is extended.
  • Laser Coaching: Shows you in your zone of genius, coaching through problems ALL students have. This is where belief is built — they see the transformation happening live.
  • Repitch: Reinforces the offer for logical buyers who need more processing time. "Never end on an offer — it's dangerous."

The Complete PHD Flow

Method Sessions (Parts 1-4)
Teaching a TASTE of the method, not the full method. Each session covers one piece of the framework, building anticipation and belief that the full method works. 21 pointers warning: "In three sessions under 2 hours, I saw 21 pointers. Less is more. Most people overteach."
Celebrity Keynotes
Morning Day 2 is the primary slot. Possibly after lunch Day 2 or Day 3 to hold the audience. Used strategically for retention, not as the main draw.
The Welcome Celebration
"The train is leaving the station. The program starts HERE at this event." Has outperformed ANY other bonus or CTA over the last 10 years. This single reframe — that mastery begins NOW, not later — is the most powerful enrollment driver.
The Tipping Point
Happens halfway through Day 2. The audience shifts from "I can't wait to get started" to "I wish I had help getting started." This is the inflection point where desire converts to need.
Pre-pitch Session
The "Show me the money/results" session. Connects all the dots before the offer is made. Attendees see the complete picture of what's possible and what others have achieved.
The Trifecta: Panel & Invitation → Laser Coaching → Repitch
The main conversion sequence. 6 steps to the offer session, 4 steps to laser coaching, 6 steps to repitch. Each component has a proven formula.
Key Principle: Sell them on BELIEF they can do it first. "If they don't believe they can do it, why would they pay you to help?"

"Elegant moves. We want them so immersed, so engaged, so excited. The thing you have to sell them on more than anything is the belief that they can do it."

Method Design: The 6-Step Maximum

Methods should have no more than 6 steps. This is a hard ceiling, not a guideline.

When you have more than 6:

  • Bucket — group related steps under one umbrella
  • Collapse — merge steps that naturally flow together
  • Eliminate — remove steps that aren't essential, and create a separate product for them

The 6-step maximum exists because 6 steps layer perfectly into the PHD flow. Each method session at the event covers 1-2 steps, giving the audience a complete taste without the full depth.

"The minute you get into more, I always bucket them and collapse them."

Personal Growth vs. Business Summit Psychology

Personal growth audiences expect things for free, especially the healer market. This is the fundamental challenge of selling transformational products.

But Bari reframes this powerfully: "When you pay, you pay attention. It's a SERVICE to get them to buy."

Business Marketing

Dimes to dollars. Rational decision-making. ROI is straightforward to articulate.

Personal Growth Marketing

Emotional decision-making. Harder to quantify ROI. Free expectation is cultural.

Key Insight: Manifesting mastery does 3x the sales of spiritual mastery — because you tie it back to money, abundance, and tangible goals.

"It's actually a service to them to get them to buy. If I spend 10 grand, I'm more likely to show up than if I spend nothing."

Global Audience Adaptation

The accordion model: shorten the days but make them more intentional. "A shorter letter is harder to write" — compressing content requires more skill, not less.

  • Time zone rooms with MC champions — dedicated rooms per region with local hosts who keep energy high
  • "Reset your body clock" messaging — reframe the inconvenience: "You don't have to fly across the pond"
  • 11 AM Eastern is the best start time for US-based virtual events
  • Simulated live replay using JD's Zoom feature for asynchronous audiences

Bari has personally run events from Florida for Australian audiences — the virtual format makes global reach genuinely possible.

"To give a short day is harder than a long day to get the same result. But it's doable."

Scaling 10K to 100K Attendees

The infrastructure approach:

  • Load balance rooms at 500 per room — this is the sweet spot for managed engagement
  • Software groups same people together = community inside community. Attendees build relationships within their cohort.
  • Bot moderation for high volume chat — at scale, human-only moderation is impossible
Key Reframe: "No intimacy issue at virtual events — everyone has front row seats." The virtual format is an advantage, not a limitation.

"If you know how to work the cameras and the virtual room, it's gonna be a deeply immersive event where they feel like all the speakers are talking directly to them."

Competitors Copying Your Model

The Grant Cardone case study: He launched "Unbreakable" at the exact same time as Tony/Dean's "Unshakable" — same topic, direct hit, clearly a copy.

Grant flopped. Tony/Dean had their best summit ever.

Why? "He was copying something that wasn't his, wasn't the right voice for him, wasn't in sync with his audience."

The true moat against copycats is not secrecy. It's:

  • Voice — how only you can say it
  • Community — the tribe that trusts you specifically
  • Like-mindedness — shared values that can't be faked
  • Big Y (Why) — your authentic purpose
  • Non-negotiables — what you stand for
  • Method — your proprietary framework

"There are 8 other AI speakers, but there's no one like you doing what you do the way you do it."

Sell What They Want, Give What They Need

Students will tell you they need "funnels, SEO, traffic" — but the real need is usually messaging clarity.

"If you spent an hour with your right-fit client, by the end you'd have convinced them they need something different than they thought."

Surveys tell you what people THINK they need. Your job as the expert is to figure out the actual solution and bridge the gap between their perceived need and the real one.

"What they'll tell you they need, and what you know they need, are often two different things. But we're gonna sell them what they want, and then give them what they need."

The Pruning Question (Beyond Summits)

Bari posed a profound strategic question: "If this was your last year, is your campus a good representation of who you are?"

The rose pruning metaphor: "I always have to cut them before I'm ready. If I don't trim them back, I'm not gonna get the best bloom."

The exercise: Craft the IDEAL path for a family member — what would you recommend, in what order, including what would be left behind? This reveals what truly matters in your product ecosystem.

"Our businesses are a lot like gardens. They need pruning. Now is a good time to think about what needs to be pruned and what needs more light."

Speaker Contract Enforcement

Bari's rules are absolute:

  • NO last-minute slides. Period.
  • NO improv. "This is a highly produced production with a sole goal. There is no improv."
  • Contract dictates slide submission deadlines — it's in writing, not a suggestion

If something breaks late (e.g., a major AI frontier announcement), speakers can update but must run it by the team first. No unilateral changes.

The hiring litmus test: If a speaker says "I'll only give slides last minute" — don't hire them. No matter how famous they are.

"Even the most amazing speaker can be a fail without the proper preparation and execution."
