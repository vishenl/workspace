THE EVENT REDESIGN

The 3-Day Event Formula

From scattered buffet to a strategic mastery arc. A proven cadence that turns attendees into buyers — ethically, lovingly, and without feeling salesy.

WHAT'S BROKEN

Current Approach Problems

Why the Current Model Underperforms

  • Buffet-style content: 12+ speakers each doing their own thing, no unified method
  • Wrong pitch timing: Rapid 30-min pitch end of Day 2, then 1-hour deeper pitch Day 3 (this is backwards)
  • Attendance drops Day 2 & 3: Audience is 40+ women with families — weekends are family time
  • Published agenda gives permission to skip: "Oh I'll just come back for that speaker"
  • No continuity: Miss a session, no impact — it's pick-and-choose
  • Day 3 re-pitch feels salesy: Going through the same pitch deck again but "in more detail"
  • Logical buyers don't have enough time to ask questions and process

"The minute I heard 'more show up for day three than day two,' it's like 'Hey, I'm really busy, can't make this day, but I'll pop in for day three.' We don't want that."

THE PROVEN CADENCE

The 3-Day Event Cadence

Each day has a specific strategic purpose. The sequence is non-negotiable — content earns the right to sell, tension creates demand, and Q&A closes the logical buyers.

Day 1: Content
→
Day 2: Main Pitch
→
Day 3: Q&A Close
DAY 1

Content, Connection, Community

NO SELLING on Day 1. Pure value. The entire day is designed to earn the right to sell on Day 2.

Strategic Goals

  • Build emotional connection to the method and the community
  • Establish the "one belief" — demystify the topic completely
  • Community building: homework, Facebook group engagement, chat interaction
  • Create continuity so attendees feel they must come back tomorrow

AI Summit Example

  • Day 1 goal: "She believes she needs AI and that it's a human approach to being more human"

"By the end of Day 1, our only goal was she is like, 'I do need AI. It is a human approach to doing what I need, which is to be more human.'"

What the Right-Fit Client Should Be Saying After Day 1

"Yesterday's session opened my eyes."
"This isn't about replacing me, it's amplifying my impact."
"I appreciate the opportunity to break AI down into tangible pieces."
DAY 2

Build Tension → Main Offer Pitch

Continue building value with more content. Tension builds naturally as attendees realize the depth of the opportunity: "This is so much information. I love it, but I need help."

Strategic Goals

  • Continue delivering transformational content that deepens the method
  • Let tension build organically — the audience feels the gap between what they're learning and what they can implement alone
  • The offer comes at the RIGHT moment in the afternoon
  • This is the MAIN detailed pitch (not Day 3)
  • Emotional buyers convert here: "Great, where's my credit card?"

Key Transition Language

"There's only so much I can do in [timeframe], so I created this."

"How many of you are loving this content? I wish you could just have help implementing it. Well, there's only so much I could teach in 3 days, but my program will help you master this framework."

DAY 3

Scripted Q&A Repitch (15 Min Total CTAs)

NOT a re-pitch with the same deck. NEVER pull out the offer deck again. This day is designed to close the logical buyers who need their questions answered before committing.

The Opening Script

"My team tells me you have some questions. 100 of you do, 1,000 of you do, so if it's okay with you, I'm just gonna take a few minutes and grab them, and then we're diving into hot content."

How It Works

  • Answer the TOP questions — each answer naturally re-pitches 2–3 deliverables without feeling like a pitch
  • Total CTA time: only 15 minutes across the whole day, strategically placed
  • The heart-close story: designed to get logical buyers out of their head and into their heart
  • Logical buyers who didn't buy Day 2 get their questions answered and convert

Example: Answering a Question That Sells

"A lot of you asked about mastery days. So they start at 11, we go until 5, going deep on the topic. Part of the formula isn't just me talking AT you — we're doing it together. You leave with a to-DONE list, not a to-do list."

"If it's designed properly, you're gonna be repitching all day, and it's not gonna feel like that."

BUYER PSYCHOLOGY

Emotional vs. Logical Buyers

Emotional Buyers

Convert on Day 2

  • "Love it, shoot it, kill it, bring it home, let's go."
  • Credit card out immediately
  • Need excitement and vision
  • Tend to be men (according to Bari's research)
  • Convert during the pitch itself
  • Decision is fast and instinctive

Logical Buyers

Convert on Day 3

  • "Sounds awesome, but can I ask you a few questions?"
  • Gather information first
  • Ask questions, process decisions
  • 60%+ of MV audience (women)
  • Need Q&A time and heart-close stories
  • Convert after their concerns are addressed

Key Insight: 60% of Mindvalley buyers are women. Women are logical buyers. They gather information. The current model only has a Day 2 pitch window, meaning we're ONLY capturing emotional buyers and losing the majority.

"A logical buyer's Achilles heel is they're terrible decision makers. Left to their own devices, they will literally swirl and swirl. They need someone to help bring it to a close."

Key Rules

Promise the Time Commitment
"I promise we'll be done in an hour. You give me focus, I give you a bonus."
🎯
Drop the Offer by Minute 45
Completely done with the main presentation in 60 minutes. The extra 15–30 minutes is Q&A AFTER the main session.
🔁
Every Q&A Answer Ties Back to the Product
Each question answered is an opportunity to naturally re-pitch a feature or deliverable without pulling out a slide deck.
✂
Don't Teach Too Much
Teach one simple concept and go deep but fast. Give them a sense of accomplishment, but only give them THIS much of a plan that is THIS big.

CRITICAL CHANGE

Stop Publishing the Agenda

Before: Detailed Schedule

  • Hour-by-hour speaker schedule published on landing page
  • Specific time slots for each session
  • Detailed topic descriptions per speaker
  • Attendees cherry-pick which sessions to attend
  • "Oh, I only need to show up at 3pm for that one speaker"

After: Curated Mystery

  • "These are the speakers who will be there this week"
  • "These are the topics we'll cover"
  • "We start at 11, end at 8"
  • "To miss a little is to miss a lot — every session builds on the one before"
  • Continuity creates FOMO for every session

The question behind the question: When a customer asks "Can I have the schedule?" what they're really asking is: "Can I have the schedule so I can decide what to skip?" We never want that.

What to Say Instead

"Trust me. I've been doing this for a really long time. I've reserved the right to make modifications. I'm not going to give you all the details, but here's who and what."

"A customer always asks for the schedule. The question behind the question is: 'Can I have the schedule so I can decide what to skip?' We never want that."

COUNTER-INTUITIVE INSIGHT

Making Summits Longer, Not Shorter

The solution to attendance drop-off is NOT compressing to 2 days. When content has a through-line and genuine value density, people stay.

Value Keeps Them

"If you're delivering great value, they'll come and they'll stay." The issue isn't duration — it's through-line and value density.

Continuity Creates FOMO

When content is method-based, missing a session creates real fear of missing out: "I missed step 3! I NEED that to do step 4!"

Scheduling Insight: Consider starting Thursday for a 3-day event (Thu–Sat instead of Fri–Sun) based on the audience's reality: weekends are family time for 40+ women with children.

HOUR 4 INSIGHT

Why Hybrid Events Don't Work

Bari and her husband tested every configuration of hybrid events post-COVID. The verdict is clear.

The Jimmy Fallon Analogy

When you go to a late night show, you're prepped by handlers. They hold up signs — "laugh," "applause." Jimmy Fallon never looks at the studio audience. He only talks to the camera, because there are millions at home and 300 in the studio.

But most speakers in our world? Put an in-person audience in front of them and they'll pander to it. They cannot help themselves. They're like moths drawn to a light. The virtual audience instantly becomes a passive viewing experience.

What They Tried (All Failed)

  • Two different MCs — one in-person, one virtual
  • Special programming for virtual during lunch breaks
  • Different content tracks for each audience
  • Games and engagement tools for virtual

"When you did all the things, conversions went up a little bit, but the expense and exhaustion didn't counterbalance the uptick."

The Rule

  • In-person performs better by itself
  • Virtual performs better by itself
  • A virtual event worked during COVID because there was NO in-person audience
  • A 90-minute lunch break is fine in person — it's an eternity for a virtual audience

Pull them apart. Each will inherently do better.

"We don't really believe in hybrids. When COVID hit, virtual worked because there was no in-person audience. As we tested hybrids with every possible control, it never worked well. Lower conversion rates on BOTH sides."
