SPEAKER MANAGEMENT

Speaker Playbook

Every speaker who goes off-message costs you conversions. From curating the right people to briefing them for through-line alignment.

THE PROBLEM

Current Speaker Challenges

What's Going Wrong Today

  • 12+ speakers per summit with no unified direction
  • Each speaker tries to stand out — sometimes with contrarian takes: "Chris Dow got on stage at a social media summit about going viral and said 'DON'T go viral.' He thought he was being smart but it undermined the entire sales messaging."
  • Celebrity speakers ($250K for Gwyneth Paltrow) attract people who come for the celebrity, watch them, and leave
  • No pre-review of slides or content direction
  • Speakers don't know what comes before or after them
  • Many are brilliant in their domain but terrible at explaining it simply
  • Self-promotion: Instagram handles, QR codes for their own funnels, book mentions
  • Marketing leads with speaker names rather than the METHOD being taught

"Did they come for you and your method, or did they come for Gwyneth? If you really think she's the linchpin and they sign up to see her, they'll be like 'When's Gwyneth? That was amazing. Thank you.' And then they leave."

SELECTION CRITERIA

The New Speaker Selection Framework

Must Haves

  • Naturally aligned with the method you're teaching (they don't need to be told — their best work IS your method)
  • Can explain complex topics in simple terms (the "fourth grader" test)
  • Willing to submit slides in advance for review
  • No self-promotion agenda
  • Either already known to the audience OR represents the "transformed version" of the right-fit client
  • Examples: influencers who speak to mass audiences often work better than PhDs who can't simplify

Consider Carefully

  • Big name celebrities with large following but own methodology
  • Speakers who are famous but may pull focus from your method
  • Anyone who insists on teaching "their way" without alignment
  • Speakers primarily motivated by exposure/payment rather than mission alignment
  • Note: "The best speakers for your event may not be famous. At Garrett White's event, the celebrities were regular guys who had transformed — not celebrities."

"Very few speakers will deliver YOUR method. Which is why it's so important to know they're so aligned with your method that just by them doing their best work, it's already your method."

PREPARATION

The 5-Step Prep Process

1

Share the Right-Fit Student Brief

"Just want to remind you, here's our right-fit student. This is who's in the room. This is the outcome of the event. This is the method we're teaching."

2

Alignment Meeting — Position Within the Method

"This is how you fit into it and why we chose you and asked to be part of the program. This is the high-level things we'd like for you to do based on what we know of you and your work. Now let's talk about how you integrate it."

3

Slide Review & Approval

"Your slide deck is due by this date, and we're gonna review it together to make sure that it's aligned."

Nothing makes it to stage without slides being reviewed. Period.

4

Context Briefing — Before and After

"You're sandwiched in between for a reason. Before you, this speaker is teeing up X. After you, we have Y happening."

Tell each speaker what session comes before them and after them. Most speakers only know their own 40-minute slot — they have no idea what the arc is.

5

Day-Of Briefing

Final alignment check. Reminder of the right-fit client. Reminder of the through-line. Any last adjustments based on what happened the day before.

"Even the best speaker, without context, can upset the whole apple cart."

RULES OF ENGAGEMENT

No Self-Promotion Rules

Hard NOs

  • No Instagram handles or social media links on slides
  • No "Who's loving this? Scan this QR code for my slides" (leads to their opt-in)
  • No book mentions or book giveaways
  • No mention of their own programs, courses, or coaching
  • No sneaky self-promotion disguised as "resources"
  • No "Follow me at @handle for more"

What IS Allowed

  • Brief bio introduction by the HOST (not self-introduction)
  • Mention of relevant credentials that support the method
  • Real stories and examples from their work
  • The host saying "If you loved [speaker], they teach inside our program" — this EDIFIES the mastery offer, not the speaker's own brand

How the host uses speakers in the offer: "How many of you loved Jane Doe? She's actually going to be teaching inside this program." This makes the mastery MORE valuable — you get Jane inside Vishen's program.

TEMPLATE

Speaker Briefing Template

SPEAKER BRIEFING: [Summit Name] — [Date]

OUR RIGHT-FIT CLIENT:
[Name], [age], [role]. She is [situation]. Her biggest problem is [problem]. What she most wants is [outcome].

THE ONE BELIEF:
[The single core belief the entire event reinforces]

YOUR ROLE IN THE THROUGH-LINE:
You are speaking at [time] on [day]. Before you, [previous speaker] will have covered [topic], setting up [concept]. Your job is to [specific contribution to the method]. After you, [next speaker] will take it to [next concept].

CONTENT GUIDELINES:
  • Talk to them like they're a smart 4th grader
  • Your audience uses ChatGPT daily for basic tasks — email, research, contracts
  • They do NOT know what "agentic AI" or "vibe coding" means (even if they've heard the terms)
  • Show, don't tell — live demos and real examples land 10x better than theory
  • Connect everything back to: more time → more leverage → more success
HARD NOs:
  • No self-promotion, QR codes, or social handles
  • No selling your own programs
  • No contrarian takes that undermine the through-line
COMPARISON

Celebrity vs. Method Speakers

Celebrity-Driven Event

  • Marketing leads with: "Gwyneth Paltrow, Richard Branson, Stephen Bartlett!"
  • Audience comes for the celebrity, watches them, leaves
  • No connection between celebrity content and the method
  • Expensive ($250K+ for big names) and transactional
  • Question: "Did they come for you or for Gwyneth?"

Method-Driven Event with Strategic Guests

  • Marketing leads with: "Vishen's proven 5-step method for [outcome]"
  • Guest speakers are positioned as experts WITHIN the method
  • Host intros and outros each speaker to set up the through-line
  • "I'm going to bring in an expert on [step]. Here's [speaker], the best [topic] expert to teach it — in alignment with our method."
  • At offer time: "How many of you loved [speaker]? She's teaching inside the program."
  • Result: Speaker adds value to YOUR offer, not their own brand

"We introduced them to speakers who were consistent with our through-line, coached by us on their topic. Nothing made it to stage without us reviewing the slides."

SPEAKER ECONOMICS

Relationship-Based vs. Transactional

Relationship-Based (Recommended)

  • Free, based on mutual benefit and shared mission
  • "If you come on our stage, your job is to support us. When it's my time on your stage, I'll return the favor."
  • Builds long-term alignment
  • Speaker feels invested in the event's success
  • Most of Bari's clients (Dean, Tony, Cody Sanchez, Pace Morby) rarely pay speakers
  • Mindvalley has this brand power — "Almost everyone will speak for us for free"

Transactional (Use Sparingly)

  • Payment creates a business relationship, not a personal one
  • Speaker may feel entitled to promote their own brand since they were "hired"
  • More difficult to enforce content guidelines
  • "Every time we pay a speaker, we switch from human relationship to transactional. It causes problems." — Vishen
  • Exception: Very high-profile non-industry names (like Gwyneth) where payment is expected
